Called Shigru or Shobhanjana in Sanskrit — "that which pierces like an arrow" — Sigru is cited in Charaka Samhita across more therapeutic categories than almost any other single plant. Modern nutrition science has validated what ancient physicians knew: every part of this fast-growing tropical tree is medicinal, earning it the global epithet "The Miracle Tree."

01

Botanical Profile

Moringa oleifera is a fast-growing, drought-tolerant deciduous tree of the monotypic family Moringaceae, native to the sub-Himalayan foothills of northwestern India (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar) and now naturalised and cultivated pantropically across Asia, Africa, and Latin America. The tree reaches 10–12 m in cultivation, with a slender, corky, whitish-grey trunk and drooping branches bearing feathery tri-pinnate leaves — the hallmark of the genus. Its fragrant, white-to-cream five-petalled flowers borne in loose panicles are followed by the characteristic pendulous, three-sided ribbed pods — 20–45 cm long — that give the plant its common name "drumstick tree."

The tree is extraordinarily versatile: its leaves, pods, flowers, seeds, bark, roots, and seed oil are all used — in food, medicine, water purification, and cosmetics. In Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu, the drumstick pod (Murungakkai) is a daily culinary staple; in Karnataka, the leaves are a regular green vegetable. Moringa is now cultivated commercially in Andhra Pradesh (which produces ~80% of India's crop), as well as in the Philippines, Ethiopia, and across the Caribbean.

02

The Whole-Tree Pharmacy

What makes Sigru truly exceptional in both Ayurveda and modern nutrition is that each part of the tree offers a distinct therapeutic and nutritional profile. Classical texts discuss Shigru Patra (leaves), Shigru Phala (pods/fruit), Shigru Pushpa (flowers), Shigru Bija (seeds), Shigru Mula (root), and Shigru Tvak (bark) as separate medicinal entities with their own indications.

🌿
Leaves (Patra)
Most nutrient-dense part. Fresh or dried powder. Rich in protein, iron, calcium, vitamins A, C, K. Classical use: anemia, malnutrition, lactation, inflammation.
🫛
Pods / Fruit (Phala)
Culinary staple of South India. Rich in fibre, vitamin C, zinc. Classical use: digestive stimulant, joint disorders, worm infestation. The "drumstick" of the common name.
🌸
Flowers (Pushpa)
Edible; used in stir-fries and soups. Aphrodisiac, diuretic, and galactagogue properties in classical texts. Rich in calcium and potassium.
🫘
Seeds (Bija)
Yield 38–40% Ben oil (non-drying, oxidation-stable). Seeds used for water purification — flocculating turbid water. Classical: antibiotic, antiparasitic, joint pain.
🪵
Bark & Root (Tvak / Mula)
Most potent in pungency; used cautiously. Root bark contains moringinine (alkaloid). Classical: rheumatism, paralysis, epilepsy. Used topically for abscesses and toothache.
💧
Ben Oil (Seed Oil)
Called "ben oil" for its behenic acid content. Stable, odourless, slow to go rancid. Used by perfumers, watchmakers, and as a cosmetic base. Topically for skin and hair.
03

Nutritional Composition

Moringa leaves are among the most nutritionally complete plant foods ever analysed — gram for gram containing more protein than eggs, more iron than spinach, more calcium than milk, and more vitamin A than carrots. This has made Moringa leaf powder a centrepiece of malnutrition intervention programmes across sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Latin America by the WHO and UNICEF.

04

Ayurvedic Significance

Charaka lists Shigru across multiple therapeutic groupings: in the Katuka Skandha (group of pungent-tasting herbs), Shiro Virechana (drugs for nasal purification and cranial detoxification), and extensively in Vata Vyadhi Chikitsa (treatment of neurological and joint disorders). Sushruta places it in Vidarigandhadi gana and uses it in treatments for Gulma (abdominal tumours), Shopha (oedema), and Kushtha (skin diseases). Vagbhata describes Shigru as Agni Deepana (kindler of digestive fire), Shothahara (anti-oedematous), and Krimighna (anti-parasitic).

The classical shloka: "Shigru teekshnam katu ushnam kapha vatahara param, deepanam pachnam hrdyam shotha shula vrana apaha" — Shigru is sharp, pungent, hot, supreme in pacifying Kapha and Vata, kindles digestion, is cardiac, and destroys oedema, colic, and wounds/ulcers.

05

Key Health Benefits

Sigru / Moringa is supported by over 1,300 published studies validating its nutritional, anti-inflammatory, antidiabetic, anticancer, and neuroprotective properties — making it one of the most-researched medicinal plants of the 21st century.

Anti-inflammatory & Joint Health
Isothiocyanates (ITCs) — especially moringin — potently inhibit NF-κB, COX-2, and iNOS pathways. Clinical and animal studies confirm significant anti-arthritic effects. Classical use in Amavata (rheumatoid arthritis), Sandhi Shotha (joint swelling), Gridhrasi (sciatica), and Pakshavadha (paralysis) is pharmacologically validated.
Anti-diabetic & Metabolic
Chlorogenic acid and ITCs reduce intestinal glucose absorption and enhance insulin sensitivity. Multiple RCTs demonstrate that 7g/day Moringa leaf powder significantly reduces fasting glucose and HbA1c in Type 2 diabetics. Also reduces serum cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL — a comprehensive metabolic herb.
Nutritional Powerhouse & Anti-malnutrition
WHO and UNICEF have used Moringa leaf powder in food supplementation programmes to address iron-deficiency anaemia, vitamin A deficiency, and protein-energy malnutrition in children and lactating women. 6g of leaf powder daily meets ~14% of a child's protein and 40% of vitamin A requirements.
Thyroid & Goitre (Galaganda)
Among the most specific classical indications — Shigru is the primary herb for Galaganda (goitre, thyroid enlargement) in Ayurveda. Isothiocyanates modulate thyroid peroxidase activity; clinical observations report reduction in thyroid swelling with Shigru preparations. Note: excess ITCs are goitrogenic — dose matters.
Neuroprotection
Moringin crosses the blood-brain barrier and suppresses neuroinflammatory microglial activation — showing promise in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and depression models. Antioxidants in leaves protect against oxidative neuronal damage. Classical use in paralysis, epilepsy, and memory disorders aligns with emerging neuropharmacology.
Antimicrobial & Wound Healing
Pterygospermin and benzyl isothiocyanate are potent broad-spectrum antimicrobials, active against E. coli, Staphylococcus, Salmonella, Helicobacter pylori, and Mycobacterium tuberculosis in vitro. Seed paste and leaf poultice have classical application to wounds, abscesses, and infected skin lesions.
Lactation & Women's Health
One of the best-validated galactagogues in both Ayurveda and modern clinical research. Studies show 6g Moringa leaf powder daily significantly increases breast milk volume
