Named for the frog (manduka) whose splayed feet its leaves resemble, Mandukaparni is among the most revered herbs in Ayurveda — classified as a Medhya Rasayana (intellect-promoting rejuvenative) by Charaka, celebrated across Sri Lanka, India, and Southeast Asia as a tonic for the brain, nerves, and skin, and today one of the most extensively studied adaptogens in modern integrative medicine.

01

Botanical Profile

Mandukaparni (Centella asiatica) is a slender, creeping perennial herb of the Apiaceae (carrot/umbellifera) family. Native to the wetlands and moist tropical regions of Asia, Africa, and Oceania, it propagates by slender stolons that root at each node, spreading low across the ground in a characteristic frog-foot pattern — giving rise to both its Sanskrit name and the alternate English name "Indian Pennywort."

The leaves are distinctive: long-stalked, round to kidney-shaped (reniform), with crenate margins and prominent palmate venation — 2–5 cm in diameter. Inconspicuous pinkish-white flowers appear in small umbels of 3–4, axillary and very close to the ground. The fruit is a small, ribbed schizocarp. The plant thrives in moist, shaded habitats — riverbanks, paddy field edges, roadsides with drainage, and forest margins — up to 1,800 m altitude, preferring waterlogged soils and full to partial shade.

Mandukaparni/Centella is the classical Ayurvedic Brahmi of the south.

02

Phytochemistry & Active Compounds

Centella asiatica's pharmacological activity is primarily attributed to a unique group of pentacyclic triterpenoid saponins collectively called centelloids — particularly asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid. These four compounds are now used as the standardisation markers for all quality Centella extracts (titrated extract of C. asiatica — TECA). Beyond the centelloids, the plant contains a rich complement of flavonoids, polyacetylenes, essential oils, and micronutrients.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Charaka places Mandukaparni foremost among the four classical Medhya Rasayana herbs — intellect-promoting rejuvenatives recommended to promote memory, intelligence, and longevity. It is prescribed as a swarasa (fresh juice) in Charaka Samhita (Chikitsa 1.3.30) for daily rasayana use, and appears in Ashtanga Hridayam under Uttara Sthana as a nerve tonic. Sri Lanka's classical tradition regards it as the premier herb for "awakening the seven layers of consciousness."

The classical shloka: "Mandukaparni medhya swadu tikta kashayaka, tridoshahara vayasthapana kusthavisarpanut" — describing it as promoting intellect, sweet-bitter-astringent in taste, balancing all three doshas, anti-aging, and curative in skin diseases and spreading inflammations.

04

Key Health Benefits

Mandukaparni is one of the most rigorously studied medicinal plants in the world, with extensive clinical and pre-clinical evidence validating its classical Ayurvedic uses — particularly for cognitive enhancement, wound healing, skin repair, venous health, and stress management.

Cognitive Enhancement & Medhya
Multiple clinical trials demonstrate significant improvement in memory, attention, concentration, and processing speed with standardised Centella extract over 2–3 months. Asiatic acid promotes BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), dendritic growth, and cholinergic activity — the biological basis for classical Medhya Rasayana designation.
Wound Healing & Skin Repair
Asiaticoside and madecassoside promote fibroblast proliferation and collagen type I synthesis — accelerating wound closure and reducing scar formation. TECA is a gold-standard ingredient in European dermatological practice for hypertrophic scars, burns, and post-surgical healing since the 1960s.
Anxiolytic & Stress Adaptogen
Brahmoside and brahminoside modulate GABA receptors, producing a calming effect without sedation. Clinical trials show significant reduction in anxiety, acoustic startle response, and cortisol levels. Particularly valuable in stress-induced cognitive decline and exam anxiety.
Venous & Circulatory Health
One of Centella's strongest evidence bases: multiple RCTs confirm TECA reduces leg oedema, heaviness, cramping, and varicose vein symptoms in chronic venous insufficiency. Rutin and triterpenes strengthen vascular walls and reduce capillary permeability.
Neuroprotection & Anti-Alzheimer's
Asiatic acid inhibits amyloid-beta aggregation and reduces tau phosphorylation in animal models. Centella extracts protect against oxidative neuronal damage, reduce neuroinflammation, and enhance mitochondrial function — making it a leading candidate in integrative dementia prevention protocols.
Skin Conditions & Anti-Aging
Topical Centella reduces UV-induced photoaging, improves skin elasticity, and accelerates repair in eczema and psoriasis. Madecassoside specifically inhibits matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) responsible for collagen breakdown — a mechanistic basis for its anti-wrinkle and anti-aging cosmetic applications.
Anti-inflammatory & Antioxidant
Centella extracts inhibit COX-2, NF-κB, and TNF-α pathways — comparable to standard anti-inflammatory drugs in several models. Strong DPPH and FRAP antioxidant capacity from flavonoids (quercetin, kaempferol) complements triterpene-mediated anti-inflammatory action.
Gastrointestinal & Liver Support
Traditionally used for peptic ulcers and IBS; asiaticoside promotes gastric mucosal repair. Hepatoprotective activity is documented in animal models of drug-induced liver injury. Classical use in grahani (malabsorption) and jwara (fever with liver involvement).

How to Consume Mandukaparni

Mandukaparni is one of the few herbs that Charaka recommends consuming as a Rasayana in its whole, fresh form. The fresh leaf juice (swarasa) is considered superior to dry powder for cognitive and rejuvenative purposes. It is also consumed as a leafy vegetable across Sri Lanka, Indonesia, and South India — added raw to salads, chutneys, and coconut-based curries.

Safety & Precautions

Mandukaparni is among the safest herbs in the Ayurvedic Rasayana category and has an excellent safety record at standard culinary and therapeutic doses. It is Generally Regarded As Safe (GRAS) in the US and widely used as a food plant across Asia. At high doses or with prolonged use, certain precautions apply.

  • Pregnancy: Use with caution in the first trimester — high doses of asiaticoside have shown uterine-stimulating effects in animal studies. Culinary amounts in food are generally considered safe; therapeutic doses require medical supervision.
  • Liver conditions: Rare cases of hepatotoxicity have been reported with high-dose standardised extracts (above 120 mg TECA daily) in susceptible individuals — avoid in existing liver disease without monitoring.
  • Sedation interactions: Centella has mild GABA-modulating, anxiolytic effects — may potentiate sedative medications (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, anaesthetics). Avoid combination without physician guidance.
  • Hypoglycaemia: May mildly lower blood glucose; individuals on antidiabetic medications should monitor sugar levels when using therapeutic doses.
  • Contact dermatitis: Topical application occasionally causes contact dermatitis or allergic reactions — patch-test before applying to large areas, particularly in those with Asteraceae/Apiaceae sensitivities.
  • Cholesterol-lowering drugs: Phytosterol content may have additive lipid-lowering effects — disclose use to your physician if on statins or cholesterol medications.
  • Children under 6: Fresh juice and standardised extracts are not recommended for very young children without specialist supervision; culinary use is generally safe.
  • Duration of high-dose use: Prolonged therapeutic use of standardised extracts beyond 6 weeks should include a break period and liver enzyme monitoring, particularly in susceptible individuals.
