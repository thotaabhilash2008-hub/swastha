Manjistha
Indian Madder — The Queen of Blood Purifiers
Rubia cordifolia  ·  Rubiaceae

Fig. 1 — Manjistha's climbing vine with its characteristic whorled leaves and small berry-like fruits  ·  Fig. 2 — Dried Manjistha roots showing the deep crimson-orange that gives the dye its name

❧

Celebrated in Sanskrit as Manjistha — "that which shines brightly red" — this ancient climbing vine has served as Ayurveda's foremost Raktashodhaka (blood purifier) for over 3,000 years. Revered by Charaka in Varnya (complexion-enhancing) and Jeevaniya (life-promoting) mahakashayas, and prized equally as a scarlet dye across South and Southeast Asia, Manjistha stands as one of Ayurveda's most multifaceted and deeply researched rasayana herbs.

01

Botanical Profile

Manjistha (Rubia cordifolia L.) is a perennial climbing or scrambling herb of the Rubiaceae family — kin to coffee and gardenia — distributed across the Himalayan foothills, peninsular India, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, and parts of Africa and China. The plant climbs to 1.5–3 m via tiny recurved prickles on stems and leaf margins, anchoring itself to surrounding vegetation.

Its leaves are heart-shaped (cordifolia), appearing in whorls of 4–6 at each node — one of its most distinctive field features. Flowers are small, pale yellowish-white, borne in terminal panicles, blooming August through October. The fruits are fleshy drupes, turning from green to deep purple-black when ripe. But the true marvel is the root: a long, cylindrical taproot bearing characteristic brick-red to deep orange bark, rich in anthraquinone dyes that have coloured textiles from the Indus Valley civilization onwards.

02

Phytochemistry Composition

Manjistha's deep red roots are a treasury of anthraquinones — the primary class of compounds responsible for its renowned blood-purifying, anti-inflammatory, and skin-brightening properties. Over 80 compounds have been isolated, with alizarin, purpurin, and munjistin being the most pharmacologically significant. The plant is also rich in iridoids, triterpenoids, polysaccharides, and flavonoids.

Anthraquinones from Manjistha inhibit key inflammatory enzymes, suppress melanin synthesis (explaining its skin-lightening use), exhibit antitumour activity, and demonstrate potent antimicrobial action. The polysaccharide fraction shows strong immunomodulatory and anti-complement activity, while triterpenoids contribute its hepatoprotective and adaptogenic properties.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Charaka lists Manjistha in six of his celebrated mahakashayas (herbal groups): Varnya (complexion-promoting), Jeevaniya (life-promoting), Stanya Shodhana (breast milk purifying), Vishaghna (anti-toxic), Sandhaniya (tissue-uniting), and Shothahara (anti-oedematous). Sushruta and Vagbhata echo this breadth, employing Manjistha in Vatarakta, Kushtha (skin diseases), Yonivyapada (gynaecological disorders), and Prameha (metabolic and urinary disorders).

Classical shloka — "Manjishtha tu varnya cha stambhini raktashodhani, kandugna vishagni cha yonidoshahara tatha" — describes it as a promoter of complexion, styptic (blood-stopping), blood purifier, anti-pruritic, anti-toxic, and remover of gynaecological disorders. Its special seat is in Rakta Dhatu (blood tissue) and Rasa Dhatu (plasma), where its bitter-sweet-astringent combination uniquely cleanses, cools, and rebuilds simultaneously.

04

Key Health Benefits

Modern pharmacological research has validated Manjistha's classical applications across dermatology, oncology, immunology, gynaecology, and nephrology. Its anthraquinone complex offers some of the most robust documented activity among Ayurvedic herbs, with significant in vitro, animal, and emerging clinical data.

Blood Purification & Lymphatic Support
Manjistha's foremost classical action — removing accumulated toxins (ama) from Rasa and Rakta dhatus. Its anthraquinones promote lymphatic drainage, declog sluggish channels (srotoshodhana), and enhance cellular detoxification, supporting conditions from acne to chronic inflammatory states rooted in impure blood.
Skin Brightening & Dermatology
Alizarin and purpurin inhibit tyrosinase — the key enzyme in melanin synthesis — producing documented skin-lightening effects. Studies confirm reduction in hyperpigmentation, melasma, and uneven skin tone. Simultaneously, its anti-inflammatory action resolves acne, eczema, psoriasis, urticaria, and chronic skin infections.
Anti-inflammatory & Anti-arthritic
Multiple pathways are inhibited: COX-2, NF-κB, TNF-α, and pro-inflammatory prostaglandins. Animal studies show significant reduction of paw oedema and joint inflammation. Traditionally central to Vatarakta (gout) treatment where impure blood accumulates in joints, Manjistha both purifies the blood and cools the inflammatory joint response.
Anti-tumour & Anti-metastatic
RC-18, mollugin, furomollugin, and several anthraquinone glycosides demonstrate cytotoxicity against multiple cancer cell lines including hepatocellular, cervical, breast, and melanoma. Mollugin specifically inhibits metastatic spread by suppressing matrix metalloproteinase activity — a promising research frontier.
Renal & Urinary Health
Lucidin-3-O-primveroside has been specifically studied for anti-urolithiatic (anti-kidney stone) activity, helping dissolve calcium oxalate crystals. Manjistha also shows diuretic action and reduces urinary tract inflammation — classical indications in Mutrakricchra (painful urination) and Ashmari (urinary calculi) align with modern findings.
Gynaecological Support
One of Ayurveda's primary herbs for women's health: regularises menstrual flow, reduces menorrhagia (heavy bleeding) through its styptic properties, and is used in Yonivyapada (vaginal disorders) and PCOD-related skin manifestations. Stanya Shodhana classification indicates a role in purifying breast milk quality.
Wound Healing & Bone Health
Charaka's Sandhaniya classification (tissue-uniting) is corroborated by studies showing enhanced collagen synthesis and fibroblast proliferation with Manjistha extracts. Rubiadin has separately been shown to inhibit osteoclast-mediated bone resorption — an intriguing pointer toward osteoporosis management.
Immunomodulation & Rasayana
The polysaccharide Rubicordifolin demonstrates potent anti-complement and immunomodulatory activity, regulating both innate and adaptive immune branches. Long-term use as a Rasayana (rejuvenative) is supported by its action on Ojas (vital essence) and Rakta Dhatu, fostering deep-tissue renewal and systemic resilience.

How to Consume Manjistha

Manjistha root is the primary medicinal part, available as powder, decoction, tablets, and medicated oils. It is extensively used in classical formulations including Manjisthadhi Kwatha, Mahamanjishthadhi Kashaya, Sarivadyasava, and Chandanasava. External use on skin is safe and well-tolerated; internal use should be supervised particularly in pregnancy and high-Pitta conditions.

Safety & Precautions

Manjistha has an excellent safety profile with centuries of well-documented traditional use. It is classified among Ayurveda's premier Rasayana herbs and is generally well-tolerated at therapeutic doses. However, several important precautions apply, particularly regarding its effect on Pitta and its strong influence on the female reproductive system.

  • Pregnancy: Avoid internal use. Manjistha is classified as a uterine stimulant and has historically been used to regulate menstruation — this action makes it contraindicated during pregnancy.
  • Urine discolouration: Anthraquinones impart a characteristic orange-red colour to urine — this is normal, harmless, and expected. Patients should be forewarned to avoid unnecessary alarm.
  • High-Pitta conditions: Although Manjistha cools Rakta-Pitta (blood heat), it has Ushna virya per certain classical texts. In acute hyperacidity, peptic ulcer, or severe inflammatory Pitta conditions, monitor for aggravation and adjust dosage.
  • Excessive dosage: Very high doses or prolonged use without physician supervision may cause loose stools or mild gastrointestinal cramping due to anthraquinone laxative action.
  • Drug interactions: May potentiate anticoagulant medications (warfarin, heparin) due to platelet aggregation-inhibiting effects — caution and monitoring advised if combining.
  • Antidiabetic medications: Hypoglycemic activity has been documented; monitor blood sugar carefully if combining with insulin or oral hypoglycemics to prevent cumulative lowering.
  • Renal disease: While beneficial for urinary calculi, individuals with severe chronic kidney disease should use under supervision as the diuretic and oxalate-modulating actions require medical oversight.
  • Children under 12: Use only under qualified Ayurvedic practitioner guidance; internal use is not recommended without specialist assessment.
