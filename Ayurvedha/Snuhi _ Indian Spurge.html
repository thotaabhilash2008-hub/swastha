Caution — Toxic Latex  ·  Snuhi produces a highly caustic milky latex containing diterpene esters that can cause severe skin burns, ocular damage, and systemic toxicity if ingested raw. All internal therapeutic use must be under the strict supervision of a qualified Ayurvedic physician using classically processed (shodhita) preparations. Self-medication is strongly discouraged.

Known in Sanskrit as Snuhi — "that which exudes readily" — and revered in Ayurvedic tradition as a potent Kapha-Vata pacifier and one of the most powerful purgatives in the classical materia medica, Euphorbia neriifolia straddles the boundary between poison and medicine. Its milky latex, while dangerously caustic in raw form, yields — through rigorous Shodhana (purification) — a remarkable therapeutic agent for deep-seated abdominal disorders, skin diseases, and nervous system conditions.

01

Botanical Profile

Euphorbia neriifolia is a large, erect, succulent shrub or small tree of the Euphorbiaceae family, typically reaching 2–6 metres in height. It presents as a cactus-like succulent with a cylindrical or 5-angled fleshy stem, deeply undulate margins, and paired stipular spines at each node. The leaves are thick, fleshy, obovate, and deciduous — appearing seasonally on young growth. Tiny cyathia (specialised Euphorbia flower structures) are borne in clusters at branch tips, pale yellow-green in colour.

The plant is native to rocky hills and dry scrublands of peninsular India and is widely cultivated throughout the Indian subcontinent, Sri Lanka, and tropical Asia as a living fence or hedge — its spiny stems forming impenetrable barriers. It thrives in poor, rocky, well-drained soils with high heat and low rainfall, making it a characteristic plant of Deccan Plateau landscapes. In Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana it is one of the most recognisable boundary plants of agricultural fields.

02

Phytochemistry

The latex of Euphorbia neriifolia is a pharmacologically dense mixture of diterpene esters, triterpenoids, sterols, and rubber-like hydrocarbons. The primary toxicants — ingenol and phorbol esters — are potent protein kinase C activators responsible for the severe inflammatory and co-carcinogenic effects of raw latex. Paradoxically, these same compounds are being investigated for selective antitumour activity at precisely calibrated micro-doses. The stem and root yield a broader range of sterols, flavonoids, and phenolics with less acute toxicity.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Snuhi holds a prominent position in Ayurvedic classical literature as a Virechana dravya (purgative drug) of exceptional potency. Charaka places it in the Bhedaniya (bowel-breaking/cathartic) gana and mentions it among sharp (tikshna) purgatives suitable for Kapha and Vata-predominant conditions. Sushruta records it in the Adhobhagahara (downward-purging) category. Ashtanga Hridayam of Vagbhata describes its processed latex as a component of Kshara (alkali/caustic) preparations used for fistula-in-ano, haemorrhoids, and skin conditions.

The classical preparatory method — Snuhi Ksheera Shodhana — involves processing the raw latex through a series of milk-boiling steps (boiling the latex repeatedly in cow's milk, discarding the milk), which hydrolyses the most acutely toxic diterpene esters while preserving therapeutic glycosides and triterpenoids. Snuhi is a key ingredient in Jatyadi Taila (wound-healing oil), Nimbadi Churna, and various Kshara Sutra preparations (medicated threads used in ano-rectal surgery).

04

Key Health Benefits

When properly processed and administered under expert supervision, Snuhi's therapeutic applications span gastroenterology, dermatology, ano-rectal surgery, and emerging oncology research. External applications on the skin carry their own considerations and require careful dilution and monitoring.

Powerful Cathartic & Purgative
Processed Snuhi latex is one of the most potent Bhedana (cleaving) purgatives in classical Ayurveda — used in severe constipation, Udara Roga (abdominal disorders), and Gulma (abdominal masses). Even minute doses of properly processed latex stimulate strong peristaltic action, making precise dosing under physician guidance essential.
Kshara Sutra — Ano-rectal Surgery
Snuhi latex is a key ingredient of Kshara Sutra — the classical Ayurvedic medicated thread used in the minimally invasive surgical management of fistula-in-ano (Bhagandara) and haemorrhoids (Arsha). Clinical studies and Government of India AYUSH trials confirm its efficacy and safety in this specialised application, now performed in major Ayurvedic hospitals.
Skin Diseases & Wound Healing
Topically, carefully diluted Snuhi preparations treat Kushtha (obstinate skin disorders), Vrana (chronic non-healing wounds), scabies, ringworm, and warts. Jatyadi Taila — containing Snuhi among other herbs — is a classical medicated oil for wound healing validated in multiple clinical studies for diabetic ulcers and post-surgical wounds.
Anti-tumour & Anti-proliferative
Euphorbol, taraxerol, and friedelanol exhibit significant cytotoxicity against cancer cell lines (breast, colon, cervical, liver) in in vitro studies. Classical Ayurveda uses Snuhi-based preparations in Granthi (cysts/benign tumours) and Arbuda (malignant growths) — a classical application now receiving modern pharmacological attention.
Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic
Taraxerol, euphol, and β-sitosterol from stem and root extracts demonstrate potent COX-inhibitory and anti-nociceptive activity. Root bark paste is a classical folk remedy for joint pain, lumbago, and neuralgia — applied topically with caution and appropriate carrier oils to create a counter-irritant and anti-inflammatory effect.
Antimicrobial & Anti-parasitic
Methanol and ethanol extracts show significant activity against Staphylococcus, E. coli, Klebsiella, Candida, and dermatophytes. Classical use in Krimi (intestinal parasites) and skin infections leverages both the antimicrobial phenolics and the harsh purgative effect to expel gut pathogens. Leaf juice is applied to infected skin in traditional practice.
Respiratory — Asthma & Cough
Small processed doses of Snuhi latex have been used in classical Ayurveda for Shwasa (asthma) and Kasa (chronic cough) with heavy Kapha involvement — functioning as an expectorant and Kapha-dissolving agent. Leaf decoction with black pepper is recorded in folk practice for bronchial congestion and croupy cough.
Snake Bite & Ear Conditions (Folk)
Warmed Snuhi leaf juice instilled in the ear is a classical folk remedy for earache and otitis media. Application of the stem surface around snake bite sites (as a topical counterirritant) is recorded in rural ethnobotanical surveys — though this never substitutes for emergency antivenom treatment. Stem sap is also applied to insect stings as a vesicant counter-irritant.

How to Use Snuhi

Unlike most Ayurvedic herbs, Snuhi should never be used in self-medication. Internal use is exclusively the domain of classically trained Ayurvedic physicians working with properly processed (Shodhita) preparations. Safety, Toxicity & Precautions

Snuhi demands the highest level of caution among the herbs in this garden. Its raw latex is classified as a severe irritant, vesicant, and systemic toxin. The following precautions are non-negotiable.

  • Raw latex — never ingest. The unpurified latex of Euphorbia neriifolia contains phorbol esters that cause severe gastroenteritis, oral/GI mucosa ulceration, haemorrhage, hypotension, and potentially fatal systemic toxicity. Death has been recorded from intentional and accidental ingestion of raw latex.
  • Eye contact — medical emergency. Latex splashed in the eye causes acute chemical conjunctivitis, corneal damage, and, without immediate irrigation, permanent vision loss. Handle the plant with gloves; wash hands thoroughly; never touch eyes after contact.
  • Skin burns. Direct contact of raw latex with skin causes chemical burns, blistering, and dermatitis. Wear protective gloves when handling the plant or its cut stems.
  • Pregnancy — strictly contraindicated. Snuhi is a potent uterine stimulant and abortifacient. All forms — internal or topical over large areas — are absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy.
  • Children: All internal preparations and strong topical applications are contraindicated in children without specialist supervision.
  • Pitta-dominant constitutions: Ushna, tikshna qualities severely aggravate Pitta — contraindicated in bleeding disorders, peptic ulcers, inflammatory bowel disease, and high-Pitta states.
  • Cardiac & renal disease: Systemic absorption of diterpene esters can affect cardiac conduction and renal tubular function — contraindicated in known cardiac and kidney disease.
  • Drug interactions: Potent purgative action may accelerate transit and reduce absorption of oral medications taken simultaneously — always allow a 2-hour gap from other medications.
  • Shodhana is non-negotiable: Classical purification (minimum 7 cycles of latex boiled in milk) is not a tradition to be skipped — it is a toxicological necessity that hydrolyses acute-phase irritants. Only use preparations from qualified Ayurvedic pharmacies with documented Shodhana processes.
