Rohini
Katuki — The Bitter Healer of the Himalayas
Picrorrhiza kurroa Royle ex Benth.  ·  Syn. Picrorhiza kurroa

Fig. 1 — Rohini nestled among alpine rocks at 3,000–5,000 m, bearing its characteristic spike of pale flowers  ·  Fig. 2 — Sun-dried Rohini rhizomes (Katuki), the primary medicinal part of the plant

❧

In Sanskrit, Rohini means "she who ascends" — a fitting name for a herb that thrives only on the harshest Himalayan heights, between 3,000 and 5,000 metres. Revered in classical Ayurveda as one of the supreme Tikta dravyas (bitter medicines), Rohini has been the cornerstone of liver, fever, and immune therapies since the age of Charaka — a tiny alpine herb with a pharmacological profile that continues to astound modern hepatologists.

01

Botanical Profile

Picrorrhiza kurroa is a small, perennial herb of the Plantaginaceae family (formerly Scrophulariaceae), endemic to the alpine and sub-alpine zones of the northwestern Himalayas — from Kashmir through Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Nepal, and Bhutan. Growing at altitudes of 3,000–5,000 m, the plant forms low rosettes of toothed, spoon-shaped leaves, rarely exceeding 15 cm in height. The rootstock (rhizome) — the primary medicinal part — is horizontal, cylindrical, woody, and intensely bitter, covered with dark-brown corky bark and bearing leaf scars at regular intervals.

Flowers are pale blue to white, borne in dense terminal spikes 5–10 cm long, appearing June through August. The fruit is a small, acute capsule. The plant reproduces both by seed and by rhizome offsets. Due to heavy over-harvesting and habitat fragmentation, P. kurroa is now listed as a vulnerable species on the IUCN Red List and is protected under Appendix II of CITES — making sustainable sourcing and cultivation a pressing conservation priority.

02

Phytochemistry Composition

Rohini's extraordinary pharmacological profile is anchored in two principal iridoid glycoside complexes: picrosides (I, II, III) and kutkoside — bitter secoiridoids unique to the genus Picrorrhiza. Picroside I is a catalpol ester with vanillic acid; Picroside II (also called androsin) bears a p-methoxycinnamic acid moiety. These compounds, collectively called kutkin (picrosides I + II), are the most thoroughly studied hepatoprotective plant constituents in Ayurvedic medicine. The rhizome also contains cucurbitacin glycosides (potent anti-inflammatory and antitumour agents), apocynin (acetovanillone — a NADPH-oxidase inhibitor), and a rich array of phenylpropanoids and phenylethanoids.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Rohini / Katuki occupies an exalted position in Ayurvedic pharmacopoeia, cited prominently in Charaka Samhita (Tikta Skandha, Arsoghna Gana, Virecanopaga), Sushruta Samhita (Aragvadhadi Gana), and the Ashtanga Hridayam. Charaka repeatedly pairs it with Guduchi (Giloy), Nimba (Neem), and Trikatu — groupings that underscore its role as a premier bitter alterative, anti-fever agent, and liver medicine.

The classical shloka describes Katuki as: "Katukatikta, ushna, Pittakaphahara, jwaraghni, arshoghni, krimighni, tridoshagna" — bitter and pungent, hot in potency, pacifying Pitta and Kapha, destroyer of fever, piles, and parasites, and — uniquely for such a bitter herb — capable of pacifying all three doshas when used correctly. It is the primary drug in the classical formulation Arogyavardhini Vati, and appears in over 60 classical compound formulations.

04

Key Health Benefits

Rohini is one of the most rigorously studied hepatoprotective herbs in the world — its kutkin complex has been compared favourably to silymarin (milk thistle) and liv-52 in multiple controlled studies. Beyond the liver, modern research validates its anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, anti-asthmatic, antidiabetic, and antitumour properties.

Hepatoprotective & Liver Regenerative
The definitive classical and modern application. Kutkin (picrosides I + II) protects hepatocytes against damage from CCl₄, paracetamol, alcohol, and aflatoxin in animal models with efficacy comparable to or exceeding silymarin. Promotes hepatic cell regeneration, normalises elevated ALT/AST/bilirubin, and stimulates bile flow. Used in viral hepatitis, fatty liver, alcoholic liver disease, and post-hepatitis recovery.
Anti-fever & Antimalarial
One of Charaka's foremost Jwaraghna (fever-destroying) herbs. Documented antipyretic activity via inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis and central thermoregulatory modulation. Picroside II exhibits activity against Plasmodium falciparum and was investigated in post-malaria febrile states. Used in typhoid, dengue convalescence, and chronic low-grade fevers in classical and modern practice.
Immunomodulatory
Picroside II is a potent inhibitor of platelet-activating factor (PAF) — a key mediator of allergic and immune hypersensitivity reactions. Studies demonstrate enhanced macrophage activity, NK cell stimulation, and balanced Th1/Th2 immune response. Used in autoimmune conditions, post-viral immune depletion, and as an adjunct in cancer immunotherapy protocols.
Anti-asthmatic & Respiratory
A standout finding: Picroside II significantly inhibits PAF-induced bronchoconstriction in animal models, with efficacy rivalling standard bronchodilators. Clinical observations support its use in bronchial asthma, allergic bronchitis, and eosinophilic conditions. Works synergistically with Vasa (Adhatoda) and Kantakari in classical anti-asthma formulations.
Digestive & Carminative
As a Deepana-Pachana dravya, Rohini powerfully kindles Agni (digestive fire), enhances digestive enzyme secretion, and clears Ama (undigested metabolic toxins). Used in anorexia, chronic indigestion, constipation with hepatic involvement, irritable bowel syndrome, and sluggish bowel function. Its bhedana (piercing) quality promotes purgation in constipation.
Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic
Apocynin inhibits NADPH oxidase — the enzyme generating reactive oxygen species in activated macrophages — reducing chronic inflammatory signalling. Cucurbitacin glycosides demonstrate potent COX-2 inhibition in vitro. Used in rheumatoid arthritis, gout, skin inflammations, and inflammatory bowel conditions. Classical Shotha hara (oedema-reducing) applications are pharmacologically well-supported.
Antidiabetic & Metabolic
Animal studies show significant hypoglycaemic effects through enhanced insulin sensitivity and reduced hepatic glucose output. Also demonstrates hypolipidaemic activity — reducing total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides. The herb's ability to improve both hepatic function and lipid metabolism makes it valuable in metabolic syndrome, NAFLD, and type-2 diabetes management.
Antitumour & Cytoprotective
Cucurbitacins from the rhizome show cytotoxic activity against hepatocellular, breast, colon, and lung carcinoma cell lines in vitro. Picroside I demonstrates chemoprotective effects by reducing oxidative DNA damage from chemical carcinogens. Apocynin's NADPH-oxidase inhibition may reduce the oxidative stress that drives tumour initiation — active area of preclinical research.

Classical Formulations

Rohini / Katuki is an ingredient in some of Ayurveda's most celebrated compound formulations. Its role in these preparations is primarily as the hepatoprotective, bitter-alterative, and fever-resolving component, often combined with Triphala, Guduchi, Trikatu, and metals processed in classical Ayurvedic pharmacy.

How to Use Rohini

Rohini / Katuki is used almost exclusively as an internal Ayurvedic medicine — its intensity and bitterness make topical use uncommon. Given its conservation-vulnerable status, always source from certified organic or cultivated suppliers. Standardised kutkin extract is available for clinical use and is preferable to raw rhizome for verified potency. It should be used with professional guidance, especially for liver and metabolic conditions.

Safety & Precautions

Rohini is a potent Ayurvedic drug — its intense tikta (bitter) and ushna (hot) qualities demand respect for dosage and duration. It is safe and effective at classical doses under practitioner supervision but can cause adverse effects if self-medicated at high doses. Its endangered conservation status also makes ethical sourcing a moral imperative alongside medicinal use.

  • Pregnancy: Strictly contraindicated. Katuki has bhedana (piercing, purgative) action and may stimulate uterine contractions. Avoid entirely during pregnancy and in the immediate post-partum period.
  • Breastfeeding: Avoid. Intense bitter compounds transfer through breast milk and may disturb the infant's digestion. Consult a practitioner before any use while nursing.
  • Vata constitution / emaciated individuals: The ruksha (dry) and laghu (light) qualities can severely aggravate Vata — use only with unctuous anupanas (ghee, milk, honey) and under supervision in Vata-dominant constitutions or debilitated patients.
  • Dose limits: Classical dose is 250–500 mg powder daily. Do not exceed 1 g/day of raw powder without supervision. High doses cause purgation, nausea, and gastric irritation.
  • Duration: Extended use beyond 4–6 weeks without a break is not recommended. Chronic high-dose use may deplete Rasa Dhatu (nutritive tissue) due to its intense ruksha guna.
  • Hypoglycaemia risk: Due to its blood-sugar-lowering effects, use cautiously alongside insulin or oral hypoglycaemic medications — monitor glucose levels if combining.
  • Pre-existing bowel conditions: Avoid in active diarrhoea, IBS-D, or ulcerative colitis — its bhedana action will worsen loose stools. Use only in constipation-type presentations.
  • Conservation obligation: Purchase only from verified sustainable or cultivated sources. Wild-harvested Katuki contributes to its endangered status. Certified Himalayan or Nepal-cultivated sources are preferable.
  • Drug interactions: May interact with hepatically metabolised drugs by altering CYP enzyme activity (theoretical, based on mechanism studies). Caution with anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, and antidiabetic medications.
