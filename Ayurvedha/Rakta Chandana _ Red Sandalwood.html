Rakta Chandana
Red Sandalwood — The Sacred Crimson Heartwood
Pterocarpus santalinus  ·  Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

Fig. 1 — Cross-section of Rakta Chandana heartwood revealing its characteristic deep blood-red grain  ·  Fig. 2 — Pterocarpus santalinus in its native Eastern Ghats scrub forest, bearing golden-yellow raceme flowers

❧

Described in the Charaka Samhita as the finest of the Pittahara (Pitta-pacifying) woods and consecrated in temples across South Asia for over two millennia, Rakta Chandana — literally "blood sandalwood" — is among the most celebrated, most protected, and most tragically over-harvested trees in India: a living paradox of sacred reverence and relentless exploitation, whose deep crimson heartwood carries within it one of classical Ayurveda's greatest cooling, haemostatic, and complexion-brightening medicines.

01

Botanical Profile

⚠

IUCN Status: Endangered (EN) — Pterocarpus santalinus is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List and is strictly regulated under CITES Appendix II. Its export from India is controlled by government permit. Wild populations have declined over 50% due to illegal logging for its heartwood and dye. All medicinal use should source from certified, sustainably cultivated material only.

Rakta Chandana (Pterocarpus santalinus) is a medium-sized deciduous tree of the Fabaceae family, endemic to the Eastern Ghats of Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu — primarily the Cuddapah, Kurnool, Chittoor, and Nellore districts of Andhra Pradesh — where it grows in dry scrub forests and rocky hillsides at altitudes of 150–900 m. It is one of India's most geographically restricted endemic tree species, found nowhere else in the world in significant wild populations.

The tree grows slowly to 5–8 metres (occasionally 15 m), with a twisted, gnarled trunk and rough, dark-brown bark that peels in irregular rectangular plates. Leaves are trifoliate with ovate, alternate leaflets; flowers are bright yellow, butterfly-shaped (papilionaceous), borne in short axillary racemes. Fruits are circular, winged pods (samaras) 3–5 cm across. The defining character — and the source of its name — is the heartwood: a dense, fine-grained, blood-red to dark crimson wood that exudes a striking red dye (santalin) when soaked in water or alcohol, and produces no fragrance, distinguishing it sharply from the aromatic white sandalwood (Santalum album).

02

Phytochemistry & Key Compounds

The pharmacological activity of Rakta Chandana is centred on its heartwood, which is extraordinarily rich in homoisoflavonoids — a rare subclass of flavonoids almost unique to the genus Pterocarpus. The principal pigment and most studied compound is santalin (a mixture of santalin A and B), responsible for the characteristic blood-red colour that has made this wood one of the world's most valuable natural dyes throughout history. Supporting these are pterostilbene, liquiritigenin, and a suite of bioactive flavonoids with potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-diabetic, and hepatoprotective actions.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Rakta Chandana occupies a position of exceptional prestige in classical Ayurveda. Charaka lists it in the Chandan Varga (sandalwood group) and specifically praises it as the pre-eminent Varnya (complexion-brightening) herb, the finest Pittahara (Pitta-pacifying) wood, and a key ingredient in classical cooling preparations. It appears in the Ashtaanga Hridayam among the Parushakadi Gana and the Priyangvadi Gana. Sushruta employs it in wound care and skin disease management. Bhavaprakasha Nighantu classifies it in Karpuradi Varga and specifies its superiority to white sandalwood in Pitta-haemorrhagic conditions.

Classical verse — "Raktam chandanam tikshnam tu tikta madhura kashayam, sheetam raktapittasham varnyam vishaghnama uttamam" — describes it as sharp, bitter-sweet-astringent in taste, cooling in potency, supreme in pacifying blood and Pitta disorders, brightening to the complexion, and an excellent antidote.

04

Key Health Benefits

Modern pharmacological research has confirmed and expanded upon Rakta Chandana's classical applications, with particular strength of evidence in anti-diabetic, hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and skin-brightening domains. Pterostilbene — one of its most bioavailable compounds — is among the most actively researched plant stilbenes globally due to its superior bioavailability over resveratrol.

Skin Brightening & Complexion
Rakta Chandana's foremost classical reputation — described as Varnya param (supreme complexion-enhancer). The heartwood paste, applied topically, inhibits tyrosinase (the enzyme driving melanin production), reduces hyperpigmentation, and imparts a cooling, anti-inflammatory action that soothes rosacea, prickly heat, and sun-damaged skin. A staple of traditional South Indian beauty practice.
Anti-diabetic & Blood Sugar
One of the most pharmacologically validated actions — multiple animal studies demonstrate significant hypoglycemic and antihyperglycemic activity of heartwood extracts. Pterostilbene and epicatechin act synergistically to improve insulin sensitivity, inhibit α-glucosidase, and reduce oxidative pancreatic stress. Comparable in animal models to standard oral hypoglycemic agents.
Hepatoprotective
Ethanolic heartwood extract significantly protects liver tissue against paracetamol-induced hepatotoxicity and carbon tetrachloride injury in animal models — reducing liver enzymes (AST, ALT), bilirubin, and reversing histological damage. Liquiritigenin and oleanolic acid contribute to this action. Classical use in Kamala (jaundice) is well-supported.
Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic
Santalin A and B inhibit COX-2 and suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-6). Topical application of paste reduces joint inflammation in arthritis models. Classically used in Rakta Pitta (inflammatory haemorrhagic conditions) and rheumatic swelling — applied as a cooling plaster to inflamed joints and skin.
Haemostatic & Blood Disorders
Among the most specific classical indications — Rakta Chandana is a premier Raktastambhana (blood-stopping) herb. Its high tannin and astringent content accelerates platelet aggregation and promotes haemostasis in bleeding disorders, menorrhagia, haematuria, and epistaxis. Used internally as decoction and externally as paste in bleeding wounds.
Antimicrobial & Wound Healing
Heartwood extracts demonstrate significant activity against S. aureus, E. coli, Klebsiella, Candida albicans, and dermatophytes. Santalin's antifungal properties and pterocarpol's skin-protective action support the classical use of Rakta Chandana paste in infected skin conditions, ulcers, and chronic non-healing wounds.
Fever & Diaphoretic
Classically listed as Jwaraghna (fever-reducing) and prescribed in fevers with burning sensations, thirst, and skin redness — classic Pitta-type fever presentations. Its sheeta virya (cooling potency) reduces peripheral temperature perception; the decoction promotes diaphoresis and clears ama (toxins) from the rasa dhatu.
Antioxidant & Anti-ageing
Pterostilbene — Rakta Chandana's most bioavailable stilbenoid — demonstrates superior antioxidant bioavailability compared to resveratrol (80% vs 20% oral bioavailability) and activates SIRT1 pathways associated with longevity. The heartwood shows high DPPH radical-scavenging capacity, supporting its classical anti-ageing (Rasayana) applications.
05

Traditional & Ritual Uses

Beyond medicine, Rakta Chandana has been integral to Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain temple traditions for over two thousand years. The blood-red dye — santalin — was used to colour the feet of deities, as a tilak (forehead mark) in certain Shaivite traditions, and as a ritual body paint in South Indian temple ceremonies. It remains a sanctified offering at the Tirumala Venkateswara Temple (Tirupati) in Andhra Pradesh — one of the richest temples in the world — where it is used in the ritual bathing (Abhisheka) of the presiding deity.

How to Use Rakta Chandana

Internal therapeutic use of Rakta Chandana should always be supervised by a qualified Ayurvedic physician — particularly given its regulated status and the importance of sourcing certified cultivated material. Topical use in the form of heartwood paste is the most accessible and widely practised application, with an excellent safety profile when sourced from legal, sustainable suppliers.

Safety, Sourcing & Precautions

Rakta Chandana has a strong classical safety record and is not known to be directly toxic at therapeutic doses. Its most pressing concern is not pharmacological but ecological and legal — wild-harvested material is illegal to possess or export without government permits in India. All procurement must be from certified, cultivated sources with valid documentation.

  • Legal sourcing only: Wild Rakta Chandana is a protected species. Purchase only from licensed Ayurvedic pharmacies or certified cultivated sources with proper documentation. Possession of illegally harvested red sandalwood is a criminal offence under the Indian Forest Act.
  • Distinguish from white sandalwood: Rakta Chandana (Pterocarpus santalinus) has NO fragrance — if the wood is aromatic, it is likely white sandalwood (Santalum album), a different species entirely. This distinction is critical for correct therapeutic use.
  • Pregnancy: Internal use during pregnancy should be avoided or used only under physician supervision. Topical use of heartwood paste is considered safe at typical cosmetic concentrations.
  • Cold constitution / Vata dominance: Sheeta virya (cold potency) is contraindicated in individuals with naturally cold constitutions, chronic Vata conditions, or during cold seasons without combining with warming anupanas (carriers).
  • Skin staining: The santalin dye in the heartwood will temporarily stain skin, clothing, and surfaces a red-orange hue. This is harmless and fades within 24–48 hours from skin. Use old cloth or stone plates for paste preparation.
  • Drug interactions: Pterostilbene may mildly inhibit CYP2C9 — exercise caution with warfarin and certain diabetic medications. Consult a physician before concurrent therapeutic use with prescription drugs.
  • Hypoglycaemia risk: The anti-diabetic action is significant — individuals on anti-diabetic medications must monitor blood sugar carefully if using Rakta Chandana therapeutically, as combined effects may cause hypoglycaemia.
  • Allergic sensitivity: Rare contact dermatitis has been reported with prolonged topical application. Perform a 24-hour patch test before applying paste to large areas of the face or body.
