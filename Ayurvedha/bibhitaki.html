Named Vibhitaka — fearless of disease — Bibhitaki is the Kapha-targeting third of the Triphala trinity. Its powerful astringent action on mucous membranes makes it the foremost Ayurvedic herb for respiratory conditions, eye health, vocal cord clarity, and intestinal parasites.

01

Botanical Profile

Terminalia bellirica is a large deciduous tree of the Combretaceae family growing to 20–30 metres throughout the Indian subcontinent. It bears large oval leaves clustered at branch tips, inconspicuous yellowish flowers, and ellipsoidal grey-brown stone fruits. The fruit pulp is astringent; the kernel contains a fatty oil used in hair preparations. It prefers moist deciduous forest and river plains.

Phytochemistry

Bibhitaki fruit contains a distinct tannin profile dominated by bellericagoside, chebulagic acid, and ellagic acid providing strong astringent action on mucosal surfaces — bronchial, intestinal, and ocular. Beta-sitosterol and gallic acid contribute to lipid-lowering and antimicrobial properties. The kernel yields a fatty oil rich in linoleic acid for hair nourishment.

Ayurvedic Significance

In the Triphala triad, Bibhitaki's domain is the Kapha body systems — lungs, throat, large intestine, and lymphatic tissues. Sushruta Samhita describes it as Chaksushya (beneficial to eyesight) and Keshya (promoter of hair growth), and Charaka prescribes it specifically for respiratory disorders, voice hoarseness, and parasitic infestations.

04

Key Health Benefits

Bibhitaki's therapeutic strengths are concentrated in Kapha-dominated systems — the respiratory tract, digestive mucosa, eyes, and hair — making it uniquely valuable as both an internal medicine and topical treatment.

Respiratory Health
Principal classical indication is chronic cough, bronchitis, and asthma. Astringent tannins reduce bronchial mucosal secretions; anti-inflammatory compounds relax bronchial smooth muscle. Key ingredient in Sitopaladi churna for respiratory Kapha.
Antilipidaemic
Beta-sitosterol competitively inhibits intestinal cholesterol absorption. Clinical trials with Triphala confirm significant LDL and total cholesterol reduction, primarily attributed to Bibhitaki's beta-sitosterol content.
Eye Health
Classified as Chaksushya in classical texts. Eyewash preparations reduce conjunctivitis and eye fatigue. Ellagic acid reduces oxidative damage to retinal cells in modern research.
Hair Growth
Kernel oil is rich in fatty acids that strengthen hair follicles, reduce dandruff, and promote growth. Traditional Bibhitaki hair oil formulations associated with reduced hair fall and improved scalp health.
Anthelmintic
One of Ayurveda's most trusted herbs for intestinal parasites. Tannin complexes create an inhospitable environment for intestinal worms while mild laxative action expels them. Validated in vitro against roundworm and tapeworm.
Antidiabetic
Gallic acid and ellagic acid inhibit carbohydrate-digesting enzymes and improve insulin sensitivity. Significant reduction in fasting glucose and HbA1c in streptozotocin-induced diabetic animal models.

How to Consume

Bibhitaki is most commonly consumed as part of Triphala, but can also be used alone for targeted Kapha conditions. Evening doses with warm water are preferred for its mild laxative and mucosal-clearing effects.

Safety & Precautions

Bibhitaki is well-tolerated at standard doses within Triphala or alone. Its strongly drying and astringent nature requires particular care in Vata constitutions and during pregnancy.

  • Vata aggravation: Highly astringent; may cause intestinal dryness in Vata constitutions. Always use with warm water or ghee.
  • Pregnancy: Avoid concentrated preparations; traditionally contraindicated due to uterine-stimulating potential.
  • Dry cough: Drying nature may aggravate dry cough; combine with honey and Pippali for balance.
