Fifty times sweeter than sugar yet medicinally profound, Yashtimadhu is one of the world's most universally used medicinal plants — appearing in ancient Egyptian tomb offerings, Chinese imperial medicine, Greek herbal texts, and Ayurvedic rasayana preparations — a sweet-rooted herb whose glycyrrhizin compound touches nearly every system of the human body.

01

Botanical Profile

Glycyrrhiza glabra is a tall perennial leguminous herb of the Fabaceae family, growing to 1–1.5 metres with pinnate leaves, pale blue-violet flower spikes, and deep-running horizontal rhizomes and roots. The root system can penetrate 1.2 metres below the surface. Native to the Mediterranean, Middle East, and Central Asia, it has been cultivated in India, China, and Europe for at least 3,000 years. The dried, peeled or unpeeled roots are the medicinal part.

02

Phytochemistry Composition

Glycyrrhizin — a triterpenoid saponin — constitutes 6–14% of root dry weight and is 50 times sweeter than sucrose. It is metabolised in the gut to glycyrrhetinic acid, which inhibits the enzyme 11β-HSD2 (responsible for cortisol breakdown), explaining licorice's potent anti-inflammatory, adrenal-supporting, and potentially hypertensive effects at high doses. Glabridin, an isoflavone unique to licorice, provides potent antioxidant and skin-lightening activity.

Ayurvedic Significance

Yashtimadhu is the fourth member of the classical Medhya Rasayanas (alongside Brahmi, Shankhapushpi, and Centella). Charaka describes it as Svarya (voice-enhancing), Chakshushya (eye-beneficial), Keshya (hair-promoting), and Varnya (complexion-improving). It is the primary ingredient in Sarasvatarishta — the classical Ayurvedic formulation for the vocal arts, intellect, and memory — and plays a central role in Panchakarma preparations for nasya (nasal therapy) and vamana (therapeutic emesis).

Key Health Benefits

Yashtimadhu's range of action is among the broadest of any single herb — from gastric ulcer healing and antiviral activity to adrenal support, skin brightening, and neuroprotection — reflecting both its cross-cultural universality and the pharmacological versatility of its glycyrrhizin and isoflavone compounds.

Gastric Ulcer Healing
Deglycyrrhizinated licorice (DGL) is a licensed pharmaceutical treatment for peptic ulcers in several countries. Promotes mucous secretion in the stomach lining, inhibits H. pylori adhesion, and accelerates ulcer healing without the blood-pressure side effects of whole glycyrrhizin.
Adrenal Support
Glycyrrhetinic acid prolongs cortisol's half-life by inhibiting its breakdown enzyme, effectively amplifying adrenal output. Used in Addison's disease, adrenal fatigue, and low cortisol states. Provides natural adrenal support during periods of prolonged physical or psychological stress.
Antiviral
Glycyrrhizin inhibits replication of herpes simplex, HIV, hepatitis B and C, SARS-CoV, and influenza viruses. Intravenous glycyrrhizin preparations are used in Japan for chronic hepatitis B and C treatment and have been studied for COVID-19.
Voice & Respiratory Soothing
Classic Ayurvedic and Western herbal remedy for sore throat, hoarseness, and chronic cough. Glycyrrhizin and mucilage coat inflamed mucous membranes, suppressing cough reflex. Widely used in throat lozenges globally. Reduces bronchial inflammation in asthma.
Skin Brightening
Glabridin inhibits tyrosinase enzyme (melanin synthesis) more potently than kojic acid in vitro. Reduces UV-induced hyperpigmentation, post-inflammatory dark spots, and melasma. Widely used as the lead active compound in skin-brightening cosmetics.
Neuroprotection
Glabridin crosses the blood-brain barrier and protects neurons from oxidative stress. Liquiritin has demonstrated antidepressant activity in multiple animal models, comparable to fluoxetine in some studies. Sarasvatarishta is prescribed for memory decline and verbal intelligence.

How to Consume Yashtimadhu

For gastric and anti-inflammatory applications, DGL (deglycyrrhizinated licorice) is preferred for long-term use as it removes the hypertensive glycyrrhizin. For adrenal, antiviral, and cognitive indications, whole root preparations with glycyrrhizin intact are more appropriate, with duration limited to 4–6 weeks at a time.

Safety & Precautions

Yashtimadhu is safe at typical culinary and short-term therapeutic doses. Its most important precaution — hypertension and potassium depletion from whole-root glycyrrhizin — is dose-dependent and primarily relevant to high-dose or prolonged use; DGL preparations avoid this entirely.

  • Hypertension: Whole licorice (with glycyrrhizin) causes sodium retention and potassium loss at doses above 100 mg glycyrrhizin/day, raising blood pressure. Limit whole-root use to 4–6 weeks; use DGL form for long-term digestive applications.
  • Oedema and heart failure: Fluid retention effect contraindicated in congestive heart failure, kidney disease, and severe oedema.
  • Pregnancy: Avoid regular consumption during pregnancy; glycyrrhizin has been associated with reduced gestational age and developmental outcomes at high doses in epidemiological studies.
  • Corticosteroid medications: Potentiates effects of corticosteroids; monitor for steroid side effects when combining.
  • Hypokalaemia: May deplete potassium with prolonged use; monitor electrolytes and increase dietary potassium in long-term users.
