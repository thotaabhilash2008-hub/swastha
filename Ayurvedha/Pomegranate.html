Pomegranate
The Jewelled Fruit — Fruit of Paradise
Punica granatum  ·  Dadima (Sanskrit)

Fig. 1 — Whole pomegranate fruit crowned with a persistent calyx  ·  Fig. 2 — Cross-section revealing jewel-like ruby arils arranged in a honeycomb of white pith

❧

Revered across four millennia of human civilisation — from the groves of ancient Persia to the temples of India — Punica granatum stands as one of the oldest cultivated fruits on earth. In Ayurveda it is called Dadima, celebrated by Charaka as a superior cardiac tonic and digestive, its every part — rind, seed, flower, bark, and juice — finding place in classical therapeutics as a symbol of fertility, abundance, and healing.

01

Botanical Profile

The pomegranate (Punica granatum) is a deciduous shrub or small tree of the family Lythraceae (formerly Punicaceae), native to the region from modern-day Iran through the Himalayas into northern India. It has been cultivated for so long — at least 4,000 years — that its precise wild range is difficult to determine. The tree grows to 5–10 m tall, with glossy, narrow oblong leaves and spectacular scarlet-orange flowers with crumpled petals, followed by the distinctive leathery-skinned fruit with its crown-like persistent calyx.

The fruit is botanically a berry — a balusta — with a thick leathery outer rind (pericarp) enclosing hundreds of seeds (arils), each surrounded by a translucent, sweet-tart, ruby-red juice sac. The arils are arranged in compartments divided by white, bitter, astringent membranes. All parts of the plant — fruit rind, seeds, juice, flowers, bark, and root bark — have distinct and well-documented medicinal applications.

02

Phytochemistry & Nutrition

The pomegranate is among the most phytochemically complex fruits known, containing a broad matrix of polyphenols, tannins, anthocyanins, alkaloids, and fatty acids distributed across different plant parts. The juice is dominated by hydrolysable tannins — especially ellagitannins such as punicalagin and punicalin — and anthocyanins (cyanidin, delphinidin, pelargonidin glycosides) that give the aril its deep ruby colour. The pericarp (fruit rind) contains the highest concentration of ellagic acid, tannins, and alkaloids, and is medicinally the most potent part.

Punicalagin, found almost exclusively in pomegranate, is one of the most potent antioxidant polyphenols in the plant kingdom — more powerful than red wine or green tea by ORAC measure. It is metabolised in the gut to urolithins, which have independently documented anti-inflammatory and anti-cancer activity. Pomegranate seed oil contains a unique C18 fatty acid — punicic acid (conjugated linolenic acid) — with documented anti-proliferative and anti-inflammatory properties.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

In Ayurveda, Dadima occupies the rare distinction of being praised by Charaka as one of the best fruits for the heart (Hridyaphala) and by Sushruta as a superior Tridoshahara — balancing all three doshas, an achievement very few single plants attain. It is classified under the Phala Varga (fruit group) in Nighantu texts including Bhavaprakasha, Dhanvantari, and Raja Nighantu, where three primary varieties are distinguished by taste: sweet (madhura dadima), sour (amla dadima), and astringent-sweet (madhura-amla dadima).

Classical shloka — "Dadimam deepanam hridyam ruchyam grahi pittaghnam param, Vrushyam shukram balavardhanam tridoshaghnam rasayanam" — extols its digestive-stimulating, cardiac-nourishing, taste-enhancing, astringent, Pitta-quenching, aphrodisiac, strength-giving, Tridosha-pacifying, and Rasayana (rejuvenating) actions in a single verse.

Key Health Benefits

Pomegranate is among the most extensively studied fruits in modern pharmacology, with hundreds of clinical and pre-clinical studies validating its classical applications — from cardiovascular protection and anti-diabetic activity to anti-cancer and gut-microbiome support.

Cardiovascular Protection
Multiple RCTs demonstrate that daily pomegranate juice consumption reduces systolic blood pressure, improves carotid intima-media thickness, lowers LDL oxidation, and reduces atherosclerotic plaque progression — effects attributed to punicalagin, anthocyanins, and nitric-oxide-enhancing activity.
Potent Antioxidant
Pomegranate juice contains three times the antioxidant activity of red wine and green tea. Punicalagin and ellagic acid are the primary drivers — reducing systemic oxidative stress markers (MDA, 8-OHdG) and upregulating endogenous antioxidant enzymes in clinical studies.
Anti-inflammatory Action
Ellagitannins and their gut metabolite urolithins inhibit NF-κB signalling, COX-2, and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α). Clinical trials in inflammatory bowel disease, arthritis, and metabolic syndrome show significant reductions in CRP and inflammatory markers.
Digestive & Gut Health
Classical Ayurveda praises Dadima as a supreme Deepana (digestive stimulant) and Grahi (astringent that binds loose stools). Modern research shows pomegranate peel extract beneficially modulates gut microbiota, increases Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus, and reduces intestinal permeability.
Anti-cancer Potential
Pre-clinical data shows pomegranate extracts inhibit proliferation and induce apoptosis in breast, prostate, colorectal, and lung cancer cell lines. A Phase II clinical trial in prostate cancer showed significantly prolonged PSA doubling time. Urolithins from ellagitannin metabolism are emerging as key bioactive drivers.
Anti-diabetic Activity
Pomegranate juice and peel extract lower fasting blood glucose, improve insulin sensitivity, and reduce HbA1c in type-2 diabetic patients in multiple RCTs. Mechanisms include alpha-glucosidase inhibition, PPAR-γ activation, and reduction of pancreatic beta-cell oxidative stress.
Antimicrobial & Antiviral
Peel extract shows broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against MRSA, E. coli, Salmonella, Candida, and drug-resistant pathogens. Ellagic acid and punicalagin inhibit HIV-1 reverse transcriptase and show activity against influenza — supporting traditional use in infections and fevers.
Skin & Anti-ageing
Pomegranate extract stimulates collagen synthesis, inhibits matrix metalloproteinases, and protects skin fibroblasts from UV-induced oxidative damage. Topical application reduces hyperpigmentation, promotes wound healing, and has documented keratinocyte proliferation activity — reflected in classical use in Ayurvedic skin preparations.

How to Consume

Every part of the pomegranate plant is used differently in Ayurveda and ethnomedicine. The fresh fruit and its juice are the most accessible forms; rind and bark preparations are more potent and should be used at appropriate doses under supervision.

Safety & Precautions

Pomegranate fruit and juice are safe for most people at normal food quantities. However, the more concentrated medicinal preparations — especially bark, root bark, and high-dose peel — require appropriate caution.

  • Drug interactions: Pomegranate juice inhibits cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2C9) — similar to grapefruit — and can raise blood levels of statins, warfarin, ACE inhibitors, and cyclosporine. Patients on these medications should consult a physician before regular consumption.
  • Blood pressure medications: Pomegranate juice has documented antihypertensive activity. Combined with antihypertensive drugs, it may cause additive lowering of blood pressure — monitor regularly.
  • Bark and root bark: The alkaloid pelletierine in stem and root bark is toxic at high doses, causing nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and in overdose, seizures. Bark preparations should be taken only under practitioner supervision and never self-administered for worm treatment.
  • Allergy: Pomegranate allergy, though uncommon, exists — particularly in individuals with latex sensitisation or atopic conditions. Signs include oral allergy syndrome, urticaria, or in rare cases anaphylaxis.
  • Diabetes medication: Due to significant hypoglycaemic activity, pomegranate may enhance the effect of antidiabetic drugs, risking hypoglycaemia — blood sugar should be monitored if consuming regularly alongside medication.
  • Pregnancy — fruit (juice): Safe in normal food quantities. However, high-dose peel extracts and bark preparations are contraindicated in pregnancy due to potential uterine stimulant effects at medicinal doses.
  • Dental enamel: The high organic acid content of pomegranate juice can erode dental enamel with frequent direct contact. Dilute the juice and rinse the mouth with water after consuming.
  • Digestive sensitivity: The highly astringent tannins in peel preparations can cause constipation or gastrointestinal discomfort if overconsumed — limit peel powder to recommended doses.
