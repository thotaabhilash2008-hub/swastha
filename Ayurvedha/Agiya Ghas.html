Known as Rohisha in classical Sanskrit — "that which bestows radiance and strength" — Agiya Ghas is one of India's most revered aromatic grasses. Its steam-distilled oil, Palmarosa, prized since antiquity for its rose-like fragrance and remarkable geraniol content, serves modern aromatherapy, perfumery, and Ayurvedic pharmacy alike, while its classical applications span fever management, skin rejuvenation, digestive support, and nervous system nourishment.

01

Botanical Profile

Agiya Ghas (Cymbopogon martinii) is a tall perennial aromatic grass of the Poaceae family, closely related to lemongrass (C. citratus) and citronella (C. nardus). Native to the dry plains and rocky hillsides of India — particularly the Deccan Plateau, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and the foothills of the Himalayas — it grows in dense tufts reaching 1.5–2 m, with slender bluish-green leaf blades that are 60–90 cm long and 6–12 mm wide. The leaf margins are distinctly rough; crushed leaves emit a characteristic sweet, rosy-grassy fragrance that distinguishes it immediately from other Cymbopogon species.

The plant produces erect flowering culms with loose, spreading inflorescences. Two botanical varieties are recognised: var. martinii (Motia), the commercially cultivated form with higher geraniol content (~75–95%), and var. sofia (Sofia or Gingergrass), with a distinctly different oil profile dominated by piperitone and lower geraniol. The grass thrives on thin, well-drained, gravelly soils with moderate rainfall, tolerating drought conditions and requiring full sun to produce its most oil-rich leaves. Harvest occurs just before and during flowering — twice annually in cultivated plots.

02

Phytochemistry & Essential Oil

The commercial and therapeutic value of Agiya Ghas rests almost entirely on its essential oil — Palmarosa oil — obtained by steam distillation of the fresh or dried grass. The oil is dominated by geraniol, a monoterpenoid alcohol of remarkable pharmacological versatility: antimicrobial, antifungal, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective, and insecticidal. Beyond geraniol, the oil contains geranyl acetate, linalool, limonene, β-caryophyllene, farnesol, nerol, and a range of sesquiterpenes and esters that collectively shape its therapeutic breadth.

The aerial parts beyond the oil also yield wax constituents, flavonoids, and phenolic acids. Sitosterol, lupeol, and fatty acids have been isolated from whole-plant extracts, contributing anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and wound-healing effects independent of the essential oil fraction.

Ayurvedic Significance

Agiya Ghas finds mention across several classical Ayurvedic texts under the name Rohisha or Rohishatrana. It is one of the fragrant grasses recognised in the group Sugandhadi and is referenced in Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, and Ashtanga Hridayam in the context of fever management (Jwara Chikitsa), skin disease treatment (Kushtha Chikitsa), and as an ingredient in fumigation and aromatic therapeutic preparations (Dhupa). Its classical recognition as a Tridoshahara dravya — capable of balancing all three doshas in appropriate formulations — gives it particular versatility in Ayurvedic clinical practice.

Priyavrat Sharma's Dravyaguna Vigyana describes Rohisha as having Madhura (sweet) and Tikta (bitter) taste, Laghu (light) and Snigdha (unctuous) qualities, Sheeta (cooling) potency in its primary leaf/cold-infusion form — notably contrasting with the warming action of its essential oil — and Madhura Vipaka (sweet post-digestive transformation).

Aromatherapy Profile

Palmarosa oil occupies a distinctive aromatic space — floral-rosy with green, slightly fruity, and light earthy undertones. It blends exceptionally with geranium, rose, sandalwood, jasmine, neroli, and ylang ylang, and is used extensively in natural perfumery as both a top-to-middle note and a rose extender. Its fixative properties make it valuable in long-lasting formulations.

Key Health Benefits

Scientific research on Cymbopogon martinii and its primary constituent geraniol has generated an extensive body of evidence validating classical Ayurvedic applications. Geraniol's broad-spectrum antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective activities underlie most of the documented benefits.

Antimicrobial & Antifungal
Palmarosa oil and isolated geraniol exhibit significant activity against Staphylococcus aureus, E. coli, Candida albicans, Aspergillus niger, and drug-resistant strains. The geraniol-farnesol synergy disrupts fungal cell membranes and inhibits biofilm formation — of growing interest in anti-Candida therapy.
Skin Health & Wound Healing
Geraniol accelerates wound closure by promoting fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis. Classically applied to acne, oily skin, wrinkles, and skin infections. Studies confirm palmarosa oil's efficacy against dermatophytes — the fungi causing ringworm and tinea infections — supporting both folk and Ayurvedic skin applications.
Fever Management (Jwaraghna)
Cold decoctions of the grass have been used since antiquity for remittent and intermittent fevers. Modern studies show geraniol inhibits cyclooxygenase pathways and prostaglandin synthesis, providing antipyretic and anti-inflammatory action that corroborates its classical designation as Jwaraghna.
Neuroprotective & Anxiolytic
Geraniol and linalool in Palmarosa oil cross the blood-brain barrier and modulate GABAergic and serotonergic pathways, producing anxiolytic and mild sedative effects without dependency risk. Animal models show neuroprotection against oxidative stress-induced neuronal damage — with potential in neurodegenerative disease management.
Digestive & Carminative
Traditional internal use as a decoction supports digestion, relieves flatulence, and manages diarrhea and gastroenteritis. Geraniol's antimicrobial action against gut pathogens and β-caryophyllene's gastroprotective properties — reducing gastric acid secretion and mucosal irritation — support these applications.
Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic
The essential oil demonstrates dose-dependent inhibition of inflammatory mediators including TNF-α, IL-6, and COX-2. Topical application in diluted form relieves muscle aches, arthritic joint pain, and headaches. Geraniol's dual central and peripheral analgesic action is well-documented in murine pain models.
Insecticidal & Insect Repellent
One of the most well-researched applications: Palmarosa oil is an effective natural repellent against mosquitoes (including Aedes aegypti, Anopheles), stored grain insects, and lice. It is used in bio-pesticide formulations as a safer alternative to synthetic chemicals in agriculture and personal protection.
Antioxidant & Chemopreventive
High DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activity from both oil and whole-plant extracts. Geraniol has demonstrated antiproliferative effects against colon, breast, lung, and liver cancer cell lines in vitro, with several proposed mechanisms including apoptosis induction, ROS modulation, and HMG-CoA reductase inhibition.

How to Use Agiya Ghas

Agiya Ghas is utilised both as a whole herb (infusions, decoctions) and through its distilled essential oil. Internal use of the herb is traditional and generally safe at moderate doses; internal use of the concentrated essential oil requires great caution and professional guidance. Topical and aromatic applications of the properly diluted oil are the most accessible and well-supported uses for home practice.

Safety & Precautions

Agiya Ghas and Palmarosa essential oil are among the gentler Ayurvedic aromatics with a well-established safety profile. The whole herb at classical doses is considered safe for most adults. The essential oil, while less hazardous than many others, requires standard aromatherapy precautions — principally proper dilution and avoidance of neat internal consumption.

  • Pregnancy: Herb decoction at mild doses is generally regarded as safe; however, avoid concentrated essential oil internally during pregnancy. Topical use in low dilution is typically considered safe but consult a practitioner.
  • Skin sensitisation: A small percentage of individuals may experience contact sensitisation to geraniol — one of the 26 allergens listed by the EU Cosmetics Regulation. Always perform a patch test before new topical application.
  • Essential oil — internal use: Internal consumption of the essential oil should only be under qualified professional guidance. Concentrated monoterpenoids can irritate gastrointestinal and renal mucosa at high doses.
  • Infants and young children: Avoid essential oil diffusion in rooms with infants under 12 months. Topical application on children under 6 should use very low dilutions (0.5%) and avoid the face and hands.
  • Pets: Cats are particularly sensitive to monoterpenoids including geraniol. Avoid diffusing Palmarosa oil in areas where cats have prolonged exposure.
  • Medication interactions: Geraniol has mild CYP450 enzyme-modulating activity. Those on hepatically-metabolised medications (anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, some antidepressants) should consult their physician before prolonged high-dose use.
  • var. sofia (Gingergrass): Ensure the correct botanical variety is sourced. Sofia variety oil (piperitone-dominant) has a significantly different therapeutic and safety profile and should not be used interchangeably with martinii (Motia) variety for the applications described here.
