Bhumi Amla
Stonebreaker — The Earth Gooseberry
Phyllanthus niruri L.  ·  Phyllanthus urinaria  ·  Phyllanthus amarus

Fig. 1 — Bhumi Amla's delicate feathery branches with characteristic fruits arranged along the underside of leaf rachis  ·  Fig. 2 — Tiny spherical ribbed fruits of Phyllanthus niruri, resembling miniature Amla (gooseberry) — origin of its common name

❧

So small it grows unnoticed at the edge of rice paddies and garden paths, Bhumi Amla — "the Amla of the earth" — is among the most thoroughly researched hepatoprotective plants on the planet. Classical Ayurveda knew it as Bhumyamalaki and deployed it in virtually every liver, kidney, and viral condition; modern pharmacology has confirmed it with over 1,600 published studies, including landmark trials for hepatitis B and kidney stones.

01

Botanical Profile

Bhumi Amla (Phyllanthus niruri L.) is a small, erect annual herb of the Phyllanthaceae family — formerly placed within Euphorbiaceae — growing to 10–40 cm. The plant has a single main stem with smooth, distichous (two-ranked) branches bearing elliptic or oblong leaves arranged feather-like along the rachis, superficially resembling a compound leaf but botanically being a leafy branch. This distinctive architecture is responsible for the most striking feature of the plant: the fruits — tiny, smooth, ribbed spherical capsules 2–3 mm in diameter — hang in a perfect row from the underside of each branch like a string of miniature gooseberries, giving the plant its Sanskrit and common name.

It is a pantropical weed, native to the Amazon basin and introduced or naturalised across tropical Asia, Africa, and the Pacific. In India it thrives in moist disturbed soils, paddy fields, roadsides, and shaded gardens across the plains and lower Himalayan foothills, flowering and fruiting year-round in warm climates. The closely related P. amarus Schumach. & Thonn. and P. urinaria L. are used interchangeably in many Indian traditions, with P. niruri being the primary species in classical Ayurvedic texts.

02

Phytochemistry & Composition

Phyllanthus niruri is one of the most phytochemically characterised medicinal plants in tropical medicine, with over 200 compounds isolated across the genus. The plant's therapeutic potency is attributed to a complex matrix of lignans, flavonoids, tannins, alkaloids, terpenoids, and polyphenols — each contributing to its multi-target pharmacological profile. Phyllanthin and hypophyllanthin, the signature lignans of the species, are responsible for the majority of its hepatoprotective activity and serve as chemical markers for standardisation of commercial preparations.

Geraniin — a complex ellagitannin — is the principal compound responsible for antiviral activity against hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and HIV. Phyllanthusiin D, corilagin, and ellagic acid further contribute to the antiviral and antioxidant profile. Phylltetralin, a unique lignan, shows anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic activity relevant to urinary tract conditions.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Bhumyamalaki is classified in classical Ayurveda primarily as a yakrit uttejaka (liver stimulant and protector) and mutravirajaniya (urine normaliser) drug. It appears prominently in Charaka Samhita's Chikitsasthana in the management of kamala (jaundice and hepatitis), pandu (anaemia), and mutrakriccha (painful urination). Sushruta Samhita includes it among wound-healing and skin-clarifying herbs. Ashtanga Hridaya commends it for prameha (diabetes and urinary disorders) management.

"Bhumyamalaki tikta kashaya madhura sheeta, yakrit-pliha-vishamjwara-prameha-kamala-meha-nashani" — Bhavaprakasha Nighantu, Guduchyadi Varga — Core classical description of Bhumyamalaki

The verse identifies Bhumyamalaki as bitter-astringent-sweet, cold in potency, and a destroyer of liver and spleen diseases, irregular fevers, prameha (urinary/diabetic conditions), jaundice, and urinary disorders. In Siddha medicine (Tamil Nadu), it is known as Keezhanelli and considered one of the most important anti-viral and liver herbs. The Tamil Siddha text Gunapadam credits it with specific anti-jaundice activity through clearing kamalai (bile obstruction).

04

Key Health Benefits

Liver Champion — The Most Researched Hepatoprotective Herb in Ayurveda
Bhumi Amla stands apart from all other Ayurvedic liver herbs in having the most extensive body of modern clinical evidence for hepatoprotection. Multiple randomised controlled trials and a Cochrane-level systematic review (Thyagarajan et al.) have evaluated its effects on hepatitis B. Phyllanthin and hypophyllanthin protect hepatocytes against paracetamol-, CCl₄-, and ethanol-induced liver damage through mechanisms including NF-κB inhibition, cytochrome P450 2E1 reduction, and induction of hepatic antioxidant enzymes.
Hepatoprotection & Liver Regeneration
Phyllanthin and hypophyllanthin dose-dependently protect hepatocytes against chemical, viral, and alcoholic injury. Animal studies show significant normalisation of SGOT, SGPT, and bilirubin levels. Clinically evaluated in hepatitis B, alcoholic liver disease, and drug-induced hepatotoxicity with positive outcomes in multiple trials.
Anti-Hepatitis B (HBV)
Geraniin, phyllanthusiin D, and corilagin inhibit hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and hepatitis B e-antigen (HBeAg) secretion in infected cell lines. Clinical trials by Thyagarajan (1988, 1990) demonstrated clearance of HBsAg in a significant proportion of chronic HBV carriers treated with standardised Phyllanthus extract — among the most remarkable antiviral results from a plant source.
Kidney Stone Dissolution
Earned the folk name "Stonebreaker" independently across Brazil, India, and Southeast Asia — three unconnected traditions sharing the same indication. Phylltetralin and niranthin relax ureteral smooth muscle, facilitating passage of calculi. Clinical studies (Freitas AM, 2002) in Brazilian populations confirmed significant reduction in urinary calcium oxalate crystal aggregation and stone size with P. niruri extract.
Anti-diabetic & Glycaemic Control
Whole-plant and leaf extracts significantly reduce fasting blood glucose in streptozotocin-induced diabetic animal models. Corilagin and ellagic acid inhibit α-glucosidase and α-amylase activity, slowing carbohydrate digestion. Phyllanthus extracts also improve insulin sensitivity and protect pancreatic beta cells from oxidative stress.
Antiviral — HIV, HSV, Dengue
Repandusinic acid and geraniin inhibit HIV reverse transcriptase activity. Extracts show activity against herpes simplex virus type 1 and 2, and dengue virus replication in cell culture. While not a replacement for antiviral therapy, the mechanism is distinct from and potentially complementary to current antiretroviral approaches.
Diuretic & Urinary Tract Support
Classical designation as mutravirajaniya (urine normaliser) is supported by documented diuretic activity and smooth-muscle relaxation in the urinary tract. Used for dysuria, burning micturition, urinary tract infections, and albuminuria. Diuretic action supports clearance of renal uric acid — beneficial in gout.
Antioxidant & Anti-inflammatory
Among the highest DPPH radical scavenging activities measured in Indian medicinal plants, attributed to the dense tannin and polyphenol matrix. Quercetin and rutin inhibit COX-2 and LOX pathways. Anti-inflammatory activity validated in carrageenan paw oedema and cotton pellet granuloma models.
Antimalarial & Antipyretic
Aqueous and ethanolic extracts show significant suppression of Plasmodium falciparum in vitro. Traditional use in malarious regions of India, West Africa, and the Amazon for intermittent fevers — a convergent folk use noted independently across three continents. Antipyretic activity confirmed in brewer's yeast-induced fever models in rats.

How to Use Bhumi Amla

Bhumi Amla preparations are widely available commercially as standardised extracts, churna (powder), and tablets. The whole plant — including roots — is therapeutically active. Fresh plant preparations have a long tradition especially in South India and Tamil Nadu; dried powder and standardised extracts are preferred for consistent dosing in hepatitis and stone conditions.

Safety & Precautions

Bhumi Amla has an excellent safety profile in traditional use and clinical studies at standard doses. However, its pharmacological potency — particularly regarding blood sugar, blood pressure, and liver enzyme modulation — means it can interact with certain medications and warrants careful use in specific populations.

  • Pregnancy: Traditionally contraindicated. Uterine smooth-muscle activity has been attributed to certain Phyllanthus alkaloids. Avoid internal use during pregnancy without specialist supervision.
  • Anti-diabetic drug interaction: Bhumi Amla significantly lowers blood glucose. Concurrent use with insulin, metformin, or sulfonylureas may cause additive hypoglycaemia — monitor blood sugar closely and inform your physician.
  • Anti-hypertensive interaction: Geraniin and nirtetralin have calcium-channel antagonist properties. Combining with anti-hypertensive medications may produce additive blood pressure lowering — monitor BP during concurrent use.
  • Anticoagulant interaction: Polyphenols and ellagic acid may mildly inhibit platelet aggregation. Use caution with warfarin, aspirin therapy, or before surgical procedures; inform treating physician of herbal use.
  • Hepatitis B — clinical context: While promising antiviral data exists, Bhumi Amla should be used as an adjunct to, not a replacement for, evidence-based antiviral therapy (tenofovir, entecavir) in active HBV infection. Discuss with a hepatologist.
  • Kapha-cold constitutions: Sheeta virya (cold potency) may aggravate cold, sluggish Kapha conditions. Those with very cold digestion (mandagni) may experience digestive heaviness — combine with warming digestives like dry ginger if needed.
  • Kidney conditions: While traditionally used for kidney stones, significant renal disease or solitary kidney warrants physician supervision before use of any strong diuretic herb.
  • Children: Traditional use in children is documented in South India (Keezhanelli juice for infantile jaundice), but standardised extract doses have not been established paediatrically — use only under Ayurvedic supervision.
