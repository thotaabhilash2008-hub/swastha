Mura
Spikenard of the Ancients — The Sacred Aromatic Root
Nardostachys jatamansi  ·  DC.

Fig. 1 — Dried rhizomes of Mura, bristled with the fibrous remains of old leaf bases  ·  Fig. 2 — Nardostachys jatamansi flowering in its native high-altitude Himalayan meadow habitat

❧

Known to Charaka as Mura and to the wider world as Spikenard, this revered Himalayan rhizome has crossed civilisations — anointed the feet of Christ, sealed the tombs of Egyptian pharaohs, and formed the cornerstone of classical Ayurvedic Medhya (intellect-promoting) and Hridya (cardiac-tonic) therapy. Among the oldest recorded aromatics, its dense, musky fragrance and profound nervine action remain as prized today as in the Atharva Veda's age.

01

Botanical Profile

Mura (Nardostachys jatamansi) is a small, perennial, aromatic herb of the Caprifoliaceae (formerly Valerianaceae) family, growing exclusively in alpine meadows and rocky slopes at 3,000–5,000 m altitude across the Himalayan arc — from Kumaon and Nepal through Sikkim, Bhutan, and into Yunnan province of China. The plant is low-growing, rarely exceeding 60 cm, with a rosette of long, narrow basal leaves and a branching stem bearing smaller, clasping leaves.

The flowers are small, pink to pale purple, borne in dense terminal cymes above conspicuous reddish bracts. But it is the rhizome — the thick, woody underground stem bristling with fibrous remains of old leaf sheaths, resembling the matted locks (jata) of a Shiva devotee — that is the medicinal treasure. This "hairy" rhizome, when dried, yields a deep, complex, earthy-musky aroma dominated by sesquiterpenes and is the source of the legendary spikenard oil of antiquity.

02

Phytochemistry

The rhizome of N. jatamansi is exceptionally rich in sesquiterpenes — the chemical class responsible for its distinctive deep, earthy-musky aroma. Over 60 bioactive compounds have been isolated, with jatamansone (valeranone), jatamansin, nardostachin, calarenol, and nardosinone among the pharmacologically most significant. The essential oil content of the dried rhizome is 0.5–3%, varying with altitude and harvest season.

Additionally, the plant contains coumarins (jatamansin), iridoids, lignans, flavonoids (hesperidin, luteolin), neolignans, and sterols — a multi-class phytochemical matrix that collectively underpins its documented sedative, antioxidant, hepatoprotective, and neuroprotective actions. Recent metabolomics profiling has identified over 100 secondary metabolites.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Mura is one of the oldest continuously used Ayurvedic drugs, appearing in both the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita. Charaka lists it among the Hridya Dravyas (cardiac tonics), Medhya Rasayanas (intellect-promoting rejuvenatives), and Stanyashodhana drugs (purifying breast milk). It is a key ingredient in Maha Panchagavya Ghrita, Saraswata Ghrita, Jatamansi Churna, and the famous Tagara-Mura sleep formulation. The Ashtanga Hridayam lauds it for Unmada (psychosis), Apasmara (epilepsy), Shirorogas (head diseases), and Tvagrogas (skin diseases).

Its synonym Bhutajata — "born of elemental forces" — reflects its classical importance in treating disorders of the mind and consciousness. In Atharva Veda, it is invoked as Tapasvini — the ascetic — linking its meditative, grounding aroma to spiritual practice.

04

Key Health Benefits

Modern pharmacology has validated Mura's classical applications across neurology, cardiology, hepatology, and dermatology. Its primary reputation as a sedative and nervine tonic is supported by robust experimental evidence, with clinical corroboration emerging in anxiety, insomnia, and neurodegenerative research.

Sedative & Anti-anxiety (Medhya)
Jatamansone and nardostachin modulate GABA-A receptors and reduce monoamine neurotransmitter turnover, producing anxiolytic and sedative effects. Animal studies confirm significant reduction in locomotor activity and potentiation of barbiturate sleep — comparable to diazepam at equivalent doses in rodent models.
Neuroprotective & Anti-epileptic
Nardosinone promotes neurite outgrowth and protects dopaminergic neurons. Ethanolic extract significantly attenuates seizure activity in maximal electroshock and PTZ-induced epilepsy models. Classically listed in Charaka for Apasmara (epilepsy) and Unmada (psychosis-like states).
Cardiac Tonic (Hridya)
Mura's classification as one of the foremost Hridya Dravyas is supported by studies showing reduction in heart rate variability, anti-arrhythmic activity, and mild hypotensive effect. Improves cardiac output without increasing myocardial oxygen demand — relevant in stress-related cardiac complaints.
Hepatoprotective
Ursolic acid and oleanolic acid in the rhizome exhibit significant hepatoprotective activity, reducing CCl4-induced liver damage and normalising serum transaminase levels. Jatamansi water extract protects against paracetamol-induced hepatotoxicity with potency comparable to silymarin.
Hair Growth & Scalp Health
Among its most clinically relevant traditional uses: Jatamansi oil applied to the scalp stimulates hair follicles, reduces premature greying, and addresses scalp disorders. Mechanistic studies suggest JAK-STAT pathway modulation and improved follicular blood flow — validated by hair count studies showing significant growth promotion.
Antioxidant & Skin-protective
High radical-scavenging capacity attributed to jatamansin, luteolin, and hesperidin. Topical preparations in sesame oil are classical for Tvagrogas (skin diseases), leukoderma, and improving skin complexion. DPPH and ABTS assays confirm potent antioxidant activity of rhizome extracts across polarity gradients.
Antihypertensive
Alcoholic extract significantly lowers blood pressure in experimental hypertension models via calcium channel antagonism and ACE-inhibitory activity. Mura is traditionally combined with Arjuna bark in stress-related hypertension — a formulation now attracting clinical trial interest.
Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic
Sesquiterpene constituents inhibit COX-2 and lipoxygenase pathways, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. Significant antinociceptive activity demonstrated in hot-plate and writhing tests. Classical application in Shirorogas (headache, migraine) supported by both anti-inflammatory and vasodilatory mechanisms.

How to Use Mura

Mura is used both internally as a nervine tonic and externally as a hair and skin oil. Given its Vulnerable conservation status, quality-certified, sustainably sourced preparations should always be preferred. Internal use should be guided by a qualified Ayurvedic physician, particularly in children, during pregnancy, and in combination with pharmaceutical medications.

Safety & Precautions

Mura is generally well-tolerated at classical doses. Its primary risk is additive CNS depression when combined with sedative medications. Due to its Vulnerable conservation status, overharvesting is an ecological concern — always purchase from certified, sustainable sources. The plant is officially listed under CITES Appendix II and India's Wildlife Protection Act Schedule VI.

  • Pregnancy: Avoid internal use. Classical texts mention Mura among Stanyashodhana drugs modifying reproductive physiology — internal use in pregnancy is not established as safe.
  • Sedative medications: Additive CNS depression with benzodiazepines, barbiturates, antiepileptics, and antihistamines. Reduce or taper pharmaceutical sedatives before introducing Mura under physician supervision.
  • Operating machinery & driving: Large doses may cause drowsiness — avoid activities requiring full alertness after therapeutic internal doses.
  • Antihypertensive medications: Mura's mild hypotensive action may potentiate antihypertensive drugs; blood pressure monitoring recommended when used concurrently.
  • Prolonged use & dosage: Long-term use beyond 12 weeks without physician supervision is not recommended. Very high doses may paradoxically stimulate the CNS — stay within classical dosage ranges.
  • Children: Only under qualified Ayurvedic specialist supervision. The Medhya preparations use very small doses — do not extrapolate adult dosing to children.
  • Allergic contact dermatitis: Rare — topical Jatamansi oil may cause sensitisation in predisposed individuals. Patch test before full scalp application.
  • Conservation ethics: Mura is Vulnerable in the wild. Source only from AYUSH-licensed manufacturers with sustainable cultivation chains; avoid raw rhizome of unknown provenance.
