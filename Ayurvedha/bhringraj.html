Bhringraj
False Daisy — The King of Hair
Eclipta prostrata  ·  Eclipta alba

Fig. 1 — Bhringraj in bloom, showing its characteristic white daisy-like flowers  ·  Fig. 2 — Close view of the lanceolate leaves and flower heads

❧

Revered in Ayurveda as Keshraj — the sovereign of hair — Bhringraj is far more than a celebrated scalp tonic. Its Sanskrit name, meaning "ruler of bees," captures the plant's radiant vitality; a creeping herb that quietly tends liver, mind, and body with the same quiet authority it exercises over its luminous white blossoms.

01

Botanical Profile

Bhringraj (Eclipta prostrata, syn. Eclipta alba) is a low-growing annual herb of the Asteraceae (sunflower) family, widely found across tropical and subtropical regions of India, Southeast Asia, China, and South America. The plant reaches 30–60 cm in height, branching freely from the base and capable of rooting at the nodes wherever its trailing stems touch moist ground. Stems are green to purplish and finely hairy. Leaves are opposite, oblong-lanceolate, 4–10 cm long with a rough, almost sandpaper-like texture from their covering of stiff hairs.

The plant's most distinctive feature is its small, solitary, white flower heads — 6–8 mm in diameter — borne on slender stalks arising from the leaf axils. These delicate, daisy-like blooms give the plant its English name: False Daisy. In classical texts, Bhringraj is listed among the foremost Rasayana herbs — rejuvenating agents that slow aging and restore the body's vitality. Though commonly considered a weed by farmers, it is carefully cultivated for medicinal use throughout India. The two botanical varieties, E. prostrata and E. alba, are so genetically similar that the scientific community largely treats them as a single species.

02

Phytochemical Composition

Bhringraj's therapeutic power is anchored by a distinctive class of compounds called coumestans — most notably wedelolactone and demethyl wedelolactone — which are unique to this plant and responsible for its pronounced hepatoprotective and anti-inflammatory activity. Research in the 1970s first isolated wedelolactone as the primary driver of liver protection, and subsequent studies have confirmed its ability to inhibit inflammatory enzymes and modulate the immune response at a molecular level.

Beyond coumestans, the plant contains a rich matrix of alkaloids (ecliptine), triterpene saponins (eclalbasaponins), flavonoids (luteolin, apigenin, kaempferol), sterols (stigmasterol, β-sitosterol), polypeptides, and polyacetylenes. Leaves are notably protein-rich. Wedelolactone's ability to upregulate beta-catenin signalling in dermal papilla cells — a key pathway in the hair growth cycle — provides the molecular basis for the plant's traditional reputation as the foremost herb for hair.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

In Ayurveda, Bhringraj is classified as a primary Rasayana herb — a substance that rejuvenates the body at a cellular level, promotes longevity, and sharpens the faculties. The Charaka Samhita names it as a herb that rejuvenates hair, teeth, bones, vision, hearing, and memory simultaneously. Its Sanskrit epithet Keshraj (king of hair) reflects its centuries-long primacy as the foundational herb in virtually every Ayurvedic hair oil formulation.

In its Ayurvedic pharmacology, Bhringraj possesses a pungent, bitter taste with hot potency (ushna virya), making it warming and penetrating in action. This hot potency distinguishes it from cooling herbs like Amla, and is what drives its efficacy in pacifying Vata and Kapha doshas. When combined with sesame oil — itself warming — for topical application, the synergy amplifies its penetration into the scalp and liver channel. Rajnighantu classifies it under moolakadi gana and describes it as beneficial for skin, teeth, and eyesight, as well as useful against worm infestations, cough, asthma, and anaemia.

04

Key Health Benefits

Bhringraj's therapeutic scope extends well beyond its celebrated role in hair care. Preclinical research — and a growing body of clinical evidence — validates its action across hepatic, neurological, immunological, and dermatological domains.

Hair Growth & Scalp Health
Wedelolactone directly activates beta-catenin signalling in dermal papilla cells — the pathway governing the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle. Clinical trials confirm improved hair density, reduced fall, prevention of premature greying, and healthier follicles with both topical oil and internal powder use. The most widely used ingredient in classical Ayurvedic hair oils including Bhringaraj Tail, Neelibhringadi Oil, and Mahabhringraj Oil.
Liver Protection & Detoxification
Wedelolactone and eclalbasaponins demonstrate significant hepatoprotective activity, reducing liver enzymes ALT and AST by approximately 40% in chemically-induced hepatotoxicity models. Traditionally prescribed for jaundice, liver enlargement (hepatomegaly), spleen disorders, and chronic liver disease. The Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India officially classifies Bhringraj as a hepatoprotective plant.
Anti-inflammatory
Luteolin and apigenin inhibit COX enzymes and reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Wedelolactone specifically suppresses NF-κB signalling — a central inflammatory pathway implicated in both acute and chronic inflammatory conditions. This makes Bhringraj applicable across arthritis, skin inflammation, and systemic inflammatory states.
Mind & Nervous System
Bhringraj's cooling action on the mind (counterintuitively, despite its hot bodily potency) calms mental hyperactivity, alleviates tension, and supports deep, restful sleep. Traditionally used as a nootropic — sharpening memory, improving concentration, and reducing stress. Modern studies confirm neuroprotective properties and reduced anxiety-related behaviour in animal models.
Skin & Wound Healing
Strong antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties make Bhringraj effective against skin infections, eczema, boils, and chronic ulcers. Leaf paste applied topically accelerates wound closure by promoting collagen deposition. Its blood-purifying action (rakta shodhaka) addresses the systemic root of skin eruptions and pigmentation disorders.
Immune Modulation
Animal models demonstrate increased macrophage activity and upregulation of innate immune cytokines following Bhringraj extract administration. Wedelolactone and luteolin show synergistic antiviral activity against Hepatitis C virus (HCV) replicase — an area of ongoing pharmaceutical interest. Used in combination formulas for chronic fever and infection in classical Ayurveda.
Digestive & Respiratory Support
Classified as pachana (digestive stimulant) in Ayurveda; improves agni, reduces abdominal bloating, and addresses haemorrhoids and intestinal parasites. Leaf juice mixed with honey is a traditional remedy for bronchitis, asthma, and wet cough — utilising the mild expectorant properties of its coumestan compounds.
Anti-diabetic & Bone Health
Leaf suspension reduces blood glucose in diabetic animal models, with effects linked to improved pancreatic islet antioxidant enzyme activity. Research into Ecliptae herba (Chinese TCM use) demonstrates anti-osteoporotic effects — promoting osteoblast proliferation and bone mineralisation, corroborating the classical Ayurvedic claim that Bhringraj strengthens teeth and bones.

How to Consume Bhringraj

Bhringraj is available fresh in tropical regions throughout the year and in dried, powdered, oil, and extract forms elsewhere. The whole plant (Panchang) is used medicinally — the fresh leaf juice (Svarasa) being the most potent preparation in Ayurvedic tradition. Oil preparations are both topical hair tonics and vehicles for internal absorption. Morning consumption on an empty stomach is the classical recommendation for maximum digestive and liver benefit.

Safety & Precautions

Bhringraj is considered safe and well-tolerated when used at recommended doses. Topical use as hair oil carries virtually no risk. Cautions below apply primarily to medicinal doses of internal preparations and concentrated extracts.

  • Pregnancy: Internal use of medicinal doses should be avoided. The herb has historically been noted in some texts for emmenagogue properties (stimulating uterine activity). Consult a qualified practitioner before any therapeutic use during pregnancy.
  • Pitta constitution: Despite being classified as Vata- and Kapha-pacifying, Bhringraj's hot potency may aggravate a strongly Pitta-dominant constitution when taken in high doses internally. Combine with cooling herbs such as Amalaki or Shatavari in such cases.
  • Leaf-contact dermatitis: The stiff hairs on the leaves can cause mild skin irritation in sensitive individuals when handling fresh plant material. Use gloves when processing fresh herb.
  • Antidiabetic medications: Documented blood-glucose-lowering activity may potentiate insulin and oral antidiabetic drugs. Monitor blood glucose if combining both.
  • Anticoagulants: Mild haemostatic (rakta-stambhana) properties in Ayurvedic classification — paradoxically, high doses of extract could theoretically interact with anticoagulant medications. Monitor if combining with warfarin or similar drugs.
  • Long-term high-dose use: Continuous use for more than 3 months at high medicinal doses may begin to increase Pitta in some constitutions due to hot potency. Periodic breaks (one week per month) are advisable for extended therapeutic courses.
  • Liver disease — consult first: While Bhringraj is hepatoprotective, individuals with diagnosed liver disease should consult a physician before taking concentrated extracts, as dosage management is critical in impaired liver function.
