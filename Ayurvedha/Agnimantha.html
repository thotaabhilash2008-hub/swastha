Its very name declares its nature — Agni, fire, and Mantha, churning — for its dry sticks, rubbed together in Vedic fire rituals, would spark flame from friction alone. The same kindling power, Ayurveda teaches, lives in its roots: a tree that ignites digestive fire, dissolves cold stagnation, and burns through Ama toxins from within.

01

Botanical Profile

Agnimantha (Premna integrifolia, syn. Premna serratifolia, Premna mucronata, Premna corymbosa) is a small deciduous tree or large shrub of the Verbenaceae family, reaching 3–9 metres in height with a slender, woody trunk and a rough, greyish bark. The leaves are opposite, broadly ovate, 5–12 cm long, with a characteristic strong aromatic odour when crushed. Small greenish-yellow or greenish-white flowers emerge in dense terminal cymes, followed by small globose drupes that ripen from green to purplish-black.

It is one of the five roots of Brihat Panchamoola — the greater five-root group — and a cornerstone ingredient of the classical Dashamoola formulation of ten roots. Widely distributed across the Indian plains, Sri Lanka, and coastal Southeast Asia, Agnimantha thrives in dry deciduous forests, riverbanks, and sandy soils. Classical texts mention two varieties: Brihad Agnimantha (Premna integrifolia) and Laghu Agnimantha / Tarkari (Clerodendrum phlomidis).

02

Phytochemistry Composition

Modern phytochemical analysis of Agnimantha roots and leaves has revealed a rich matrix of alkaloids, iridoid glycosides, flavonoids, sterols, saponins, and essential oils. Its signature alkaloids — premnine, ganikarine, ganiarine, and premnazole — are largely responsible for its anti-inflammatory and cardio-protective effects, while the flavonoids luteolin, apigenin, scutellarein, and pectolinarigenin drive its antioxidant action.

Research published in Journal of Ethnopharmacology identified petroleum ether extract of Premna integrifolia roots as a potent dual inhibitor of COX-2 (IC50 = 6.15 μg/mL) and 5-LOX (IC50 = 11.33 μg/mL) — targeting both major inflammatory pathways simultaneously, a property rare even among modern pharmaceutical agents.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Agnimantha is classified by Charaka under Shothahara Mahakashaya (anti-inflammatory group), Sheeta Prashamana Mahakashaya (anti-cold group), and Anuvasanopaga Mahakashaya (adjuvant to oil enema). Sushruta includes it in Viratarvadi Gana, Varunadi Gana, and the Brihat Panchamoola — firmly establishing its role as one of the principal Vata-Kapha pacifying medicines in the materia medica.

Its rasa (taste) is predominantly tikta (bitter), katu (pungent), and kashaya (astringent), with a subtle madhura (sweet) undertone. Guna is laghu (light) and ruksha (dry); vipaka is katu (pungent); and veerya is ushna (hot). This hot potency makes it a definitive Deepana-Pachana herb — kindling digestive fire and burning away accumulated toxins.

04

Key Health Benefits

As one of the ten roots of the Dashamoola, Agnimantha sits at the heart of Ayurveda's anti-inflammatory pharmacology. Its therapeutic spectrum extends across rheumatological, digestive, hepatic, respiratory, and neurological domains — each validated by increasingly robust modern pharmacological research.

Digestive Fire Kindler
The signature action — classical Agni Deepana. Root decoction stimulates appetite, relieves bloating, indigestion (Agnimandya), and sluggish digestion. The traditional Agnimantha Pani (120g roots boiled in 480g water) is taken before meals by poor eaters and after meals by those with weak digestion.
Dual Anti-inflammatory Action
Simultaneously inhibits both COX-2 and 5-LOX enzymes — a rare dual mechanism validated in 2018 research. Downregulates IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and PGE2 in cell-line studies. Central to managing arthritis, neuralgia, and inflammatory pain without gastric side effects of NSAIDs.
Anti-arthritic & Vata Disorders
Cornerstone of Dashamoola formulations for rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, sciatica, and all Vata-vikara. Mahamasha Tailam and Maharajaprasarini Thailam — classical oils used for paralysis, tinnitus, and neuromuscular conditions — derive their efficacy largely from Agnimantha.
Hepatoprotective
Betulinic acid and iridoid glycosides protect hepatocytes from toxin-induced damage. Traditional use in liver enlargement, jaundice, and chronic hepatic insufficiency is supported by preclinical research showing reduced markers of hepatotoxicity.
Diuretic & Urinary Health
Root decoction promotes urine flow, reduces oedema, and traditionally treats chyluria, gonorrhoea, and urinary retention. Useful in combination with Haritaki decoction for persistent urinary tract issues and kidney support.
Anti-allergic & Skin Conditions
Root powder (2g) with Yashad Bhasma (125mg) reduces urticaria, hives, and itching within 1–2 weeks of use. Leaf paste applied externally soothes skin eruptions, eczema, and allergic reactions via antipruritic and antihistaminic action.
Respiratory Relief
Expectorant action helps in chronic cough, bronchitis, asthma, and Kaphaja respiratory disorders. Seed powder is classically prescribed for rhinitis and breathing difficulties. Leaves rubbed with black pepper treat fevers and colds.
Eruptive Fevers & Anaemia
Traditionally used in eruptive fevers — measles, scarlet fever, small pox, and erysipelas — and as a blood-building tonic for Pandu (anaemia). Supports haemoglobin formation and reduces fever while managing systemic inflammation.

How to Consume Agnimantha

Agnimantha is rarely consumed as food and is used almost exclusively as medicine, predominantly in the form of decoctions, powders, medicated oils, and classical formulations. Its root is the most potent part and features in several of Ayurveda's most renowned polyherbal preparations. Best taken under practitioner supervision, paired with appropriate anupana (vehicle) based on dosha.

Safety & Precautions

When used in appropriate dosha-matched doses, Agnimantha is generally considered safe with no significant side effects. However, its hot potency (ushna virya) and alkaloid content call for measured caution in specific constitutions and clinical contexts.

  • Pregnancy: Avoid entirely during pregnancy. The hot, penetrating (tikshna) and emmenagogue-like properties may affect uterine stability. Not recommended in any form unless under specialist supervision.
  • Breastfeeding: Limited safety data. Avoid concentrated extracts and medicinal doses during lactation; consult a qualified Ayurvedic physician before use.
  • High Pitta constitutions: The ushna (hot) potency can aggravate existing Pitta conditions. Use in reduced doses combined with cooling anupanas (ghee, milk, coconut water).
  • Hyperacidity & ulcers: Avoid during acute acidity, gastritis, or peptic ulcer flare-ups. The pungent-hot qualities may worsen burning sensations and mucosal irritation.
  • Dosage limits: High doses may cause digestive discomfort, burning sensation in the stomach, or mild heat-related symptoms (sweating, restlessness). Stay within 1–3 g powder or 30–50 ml decoction per dose.
  • Long-term use: Continuous use beyond 2–3 months should be monitored. Take periodic breaks to prevent accumulated heat (Pitta increase) in the system.
  • Children: Paediatric dosing requires practitioner supervision. Not recommended as self-medication for young children.
  • Antihypertensive & cardiac medications: The sympathomimetic alkaloids (ganikarine, ganiarine) may interact with cardiovascular drugs. Disclose concurrent use to your physician.
