Mahaphala
Wild Jack — The Great Fruit
Artocarpus hirsutus Lam.  ·  Moraceae
Entry 510  ·  Swastha Herbal Garden

❧

Named in Sanskrit as Mahaphala — "the great fruit" — this towering rainforest giant of the Western Ghats has fed forest communities and healed their ailments for millennia. Revered by Ayurvedic physicians for its astringent bark and nutritious seeds, Wild Jack stands as a lesser-known cousin of the celebrated Jackfruit, yet surpasses it in ecological and ethnomedicinal significance across Kerala, Karnataka, and Goa.

01

Botanical Profile

Artocarpus hirsutus (Wild Jack / Aini) is a large evergreen tree of the Moraceae family — kin to Jackfruit (A. heterophyllus), Breadfruit (A. altilis), and Fig (Ficus spp.). Endemic to the semi-evergreen and moist deciduous forests of the Western Ghats, it towers to 25–38 metres with a spreading canopy and a buttressed trunk reaching 2–3 m in girth. The bark is dark grey-brown, deeply fissured, and exudes a milky, sticky latex when cut.

Leaves are large (15–25 cm), broadly elliptic to obovate, coriaceous, glossy on the upper surface and densely hairy beneath — giving the species its name hirsutus (hairy). The fruits are cauliflorous (borne on the trunk and main branches), spherical to ellipsoid, 7–12 cm across, covered with soft, blunt spines, and ripen to yellowish-green. Each fruit contains 8–15 seeds embedded in sweet, fibrous, creamy-white arils. The heartwood — called Aini — is among the most prized timbers of peninsular India, golden-yellow to orange-brown with fine grain.

02

Phytochemistry & Composition

Phytochemical investigations of Artocarpus hirsutus have identified a rich array of flavonoids, stilbenoids, isoprenylated flavones, and phenolic acids — the chemical signature shared across genus Artocarpus but with species-specific variations. The heartwood and bark are particularly rich in bioactive compounds, while the seeds offer significant nutritional value. Latex contains ficin-type proteases active against intestinal parasites.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Mahaphala is catalogued in classical Ayurvedic Nighantu texts under the fruit-drug (phaladi varga) category. Its fruit is described as kashaya-madhura (astringent-sweet), its bark as powerfully kashaya (astringent), and its overall action as cooling (sheeta virya) — making it particularly effective for Pitta and Rakta (blood) disorders. Classical indications include diarrhoea, dysentery, haemorrhage, skin diseases, and Kapha-related digestive sluggishness.

The bark paste is described in tribal ethnobotany as a classical wound-dressing and skin-disease remedy. The latex is used in Keralite folk medicine as an anthelmintic and for healing cracked lips and tongue sores. Boiled seeds, mashed and seasoned, form a traditional medicinal food (pathyam) offered to the sick and convalescent across the Malabar region.

04

Key Health Benefits

Modern ethnopharmacological and pharmacological studies on Artocarpus hirsutus and closely related Artocarpus species corroborate its traditional applications — demonstrating significant anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antioxidant, anthelmintic, and haemostatic activities through various extract assays and in vivo models.

Antidiarrhoeal & GI Astringent
Bark decoction is the primary classical use — tannins and polyphenols constrict intestinal mucosa, reduce secretion, and combat enteric pathogens responsible for infective diarrhoea and dysentery. A traditional staple medicine in Western Ghats tribal practice.
Haemostatic & Wound Healing
Bark paste applied directly to wounds accelerates coagulation through tannin-mediated protein precipitation. Internally, bark decoction is used for haematuria, menorrhagia, and haemorrhagic diarrhoea — classic Rakta-sthambhana (blood-arresting) applications.
Antimicrobial & Antifungal
Isoprenylated flavones — artocarpin, isoartocarpin — exhibit potent activity against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, and antifungal action against dermatophytes. Bark extract is applied to infected wounds, ringworm, and skin ulcers in folk tradition.
Antioxidant & Anti-inflammatory
Oxyresveratrol and stilbenoids from heartwood show strong DPPH radical-scavenging and COX inhibitory activity in comparative assays. Gallic and ellagic acids further contribute to anti-inflammatory activity relevant in chronic disease prevention.
Anthelmintic (Deworming)
The milky latex contains ficin-type cysteine proteases that immobilise and digest intestinal parasitic worms — a well-documented mechanism across Moraceae. Traditional practice administers latex on an empty stomach for tapeworm and roundworm infestation.
Nutritive & Convalescent Food
Seeds are calorie-dense, rich in starch and essential amino acids — boiled seeds form a traditional recovery food for the sick, post-partum women, and malnourished children in Malabar and coastal Karnataka. Fruit arils provide natural sugars and vitamins.
Skin Disease & Dermatology
Leaf and bark paste applied to eczema, psoriasis, and fungal skin infections in tribal medicine of the Western Ghats. Oxyresveratrol's anti-tyrosinase activity suggests potential for hyperpigmentation treatment — an active area of cosmeceutical research.
Antitumour Potential
Artohirsutol and hirsutol — stilbenoids unique to A. hirsutus — have demonstrated cytotoxic activity against cancer cell lines in in vitro assays. Artocarpin has shown antiproliferative activity against breast and skin cancer cell models in related Artocarpus studies.

How to Use Mahaphala

Mahaphala is primarily used through traditional preparation of its bark, latex, seeds, and fruit. Internal medicinal use of bark preparations should be supervised; the seeds and ripe fruit are safe as food. The tree is endemic and protected in several regions — wild harvesting of bark should be done sustainably or obtained from cultivated sources.

06

Ecological Note & Conservation

Artocarpus hirsutus is classified as Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List due to continued habitat loss from deforestation of the Western Ghats — a global biodiversity hotspot. The tree is among the tallest and most ecologically significant canopy trees of Malabar's rainforests, providing food and habitat for hornbills, Malabar giant squirrels, and fruit bats.

Its heartwood — Aini — is heavily exploited for furniture, construction, and boat-building. This dual pressure on timber and habitat has drastically reduced wild populations. Several state forest departments now list it under protected species. Sustainable cultivation in homesteads (as part of the traditional tharavadu compound garden in Kerala) is actively encouraged as a conservation and food-security measure.

07

Safety & Precautions

Mahaphala in its fruit and seed forms is a traditional safe food consumed for generations by forest communities. Medicinal bark preparations are generally well-tolerated at therapeutic doses, but caution is warranted in specific situations.

  • Pregnancy: Medicinal bark preparations and latex should be avoided internally during pregnancy; the fruit in moderate dietary amounts is generally considered safe.
  • Constipation & Vata conditions: Kashaya (astringent) action may worsen constipation or dry-type bowel conditions; avoid prolonged internal bark use in such constitutions.
  • Latex allergy: Individuals with latex allergy (rubber allergy) may cross-react with Artocarpus latex — topical or internal use should be avoided without prior evaluation.
  • Diabetes: Seeds are starch-rich — individuals with diabetes should moderate seed consumption and monitor glycaemic response.
  • Over-consumption of fruit: Eating large quantities of ripe fruit in one sitting may cause bloating, flatulence, and heaviness (guru guna) — especially in Kapha constitutions.
  • Anthelmintic latex use: Internal latex use for deworming must be done under practitioner guidance — excessive dose may cause nausea, GI irritation, or purging.
  • Wild harvesting: A. hirsutus is a protected species in parts of Kerala and Karnataka — obtain bark and plant material from licensed or cultivated sources only.
  • Drug interactions: Strong astringents may reduce absorption of oral medications; space medicinal bark preparations at least two hours from pharmaceutical drugs.
