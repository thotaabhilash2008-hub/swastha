Kurchi
Conessi Bark — The Bitter Bark of Ayurveda
Holarrhena pubescens  ·  Holarrhena antidysenterica

Fig. 1 — Clusters of fragrant white flowers of the Kurchi tree in bloom  ·  Fig. 2 — Dried Kurchi bark and seeds, the primary medicinal parts used in classical Ayurveda

❧

Revered across millennia as Kutaja — "born on the mountain" — Kurchi is one of Ayurveda's most celebrated anti-dysenteric herbs, praised in the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita as the supreme remedy for Atisara (diarrhoea) and Pravahika (dysentery). Its bark, seeds, and flowers together form a complete pharmacopoeia that bridges ancient wisdom and modern pharmacology.

01

Botanical Profile

Kurchi (Holarrhena pubescens Wall. ex G. Don) is a medium-sized deciduous tree of the Apocynaceae family, reaching 6–12 metres in height. It is distributed widely across the Indian subcontinent — from the Himalayas and the Western Ghats to Sri Lanka, Myanmar, and tropical Africa — typically growing in moist deciduous forests, river banks, and forest margins up to 1,200 m elevation.

The bark is pale grey with lenticels and exudes a milky latex when cut. Leaves are opposite, elliptic-oblong, shortly pubescent below, 10–20 cm long. The flowers are white, fragrant, and borne in dense terminal corymbs — blooming April to June. The fruit is a paired follicle (30–45 cm long) bearing numerous seeds crowned with tufts of long silky hairs. All parts of the tree — bark, seeds, root, flowers, and leaves — are used medicinally, though the bark and seeds are primary.

02

Phytochemistry Composition

Kurchi is one of the richest known plant sources of steroidal alkaloids, with over 30 alkaloids isolated from its bark and seeds. Conessine — the principal alkaloid of the bark — is a potent amoebicide that has been extensively studied in both traditional and modern contexts. The seeds (Indrayava) have a distinct phytochemical profile complementary to the bark, rich in bitter glycosides and fatty acids.

03

Ayurvedic Significance

Kutaja is one of the most celebrated herbs in the classical Ayurvedic lexicon. Charaka lists it among the top herbs for Grahani (IBS/malabsorption), Atisara (diarrhoea), and Raktapitta (bleeding disorders). Sushruta praised it for healing intestinal ulcerations and haemorrhagic dysentery. The seeds — known as Indrayava — are listed separately as a drug of choice in fever with diarrhoea.

The classical shloka — "Kutajah kaphapittasra pravahikatisarajit, tikto graahi laghushshnashcha krimighno rochanavrinah" — describes Kutaja as pacifying Kapha, Pitta, and Rakta; conquering dysentery; bitter, absorbent, light, hot; destroying worms; promoting appetite; and healing wounds. This single verse encapsulates its primary Ayurvedic indications with remarkable precision.

04

Key Health Benefits

Modern pharmacological research has firmly established Kurchi as one of the most scientifically validated Ayurvedic herbs — its anti-amoebic, antidiarrhoeal, and anti-inflammatory properties backed by robust preclinical and clinical evidence, validating over two millennia of classical application.

Anti-dysenteric & Anti-amoebic
Conessine, the principal bark alkaloid, demonstrates potent activity against Entamoeba histolytica — the causative agent of amoebic dysentery — and is effective even against metronidazole-resistant strains. Bark decoctions remain a frontline classical treatment for acute and chronic amoebic dysentery.
Antidiarrhoeal & Grahi
Kurchi's astringent tannins and alkaloids reduce intestinal hypermotility, absorb excess fluid from the gut lumen, and restore normal bowel transit. It is prescribed across all Ayurvedic traditions for IBS with diarrhoea, malabsorption (Grahani), and chronic loose stools — including the celebrated Kutajghan Vati formulation.
Intestinal Antimicrobial
Bark and seed extracts demonstrate significant in vitro activity against Shigella, Salmonella, E. coli, and Clostridium — the principal bacterial pathogens of infectious diarrhoea and food poisoning. The alkaloid complex acts on bacterial membranes and disrupts pathogen colonisation of intestinal mucosa.
Wound Healing & Skin Conditions
Bark paste applied topically promotes wound closure through astringent haemostasis, antimicrobial protection, and collagen synthesis promotion. Classical texts recommend it for chronic skin ulcers, eczema, and infected wounds. Seed paste is applied externally in skin infections and parasitic infestations.
Anti-inflammatory & Antipyretic
Ethanolic extracts of bark and root show significant COX-inhibition and pro-inflammatory cytokine suppression. The herb is classically indicated in fever accompanying intestinal infections — its cool potency (Sheeta virya) combined with Pitta-pacifying bitterness provides gentle, sustained fever reduction without cold suppression.
Menstrual & Gynaecological Health
Kurchi is a classical remedy for menorrhagia (excessive menstruation) and leucorrhoea — its astringent and haemostatic actions reducing uterine bleeding and abnormal vaginal discharge. Indrayava (seeds) are particularly mentioned in Garbhaposhana (foetal nourishment) formulations in classical texts.
Antioxidant & Hepatoprotective
Ursolic acid, lupeol, and β-sitosterol from Kurchi demonstrate significant hepatoprotective activity in carbon tetrachloride-induced hepatotoxicity models, reducing liver enzyme elevation and supporting hepatocyte regeneration. DPPH and FRAP assays confirm robust free-radical scavenging capacity of bark extracts.
Anthelmintic & Krimighna
Classical Ayurveda classifies Kutaja as a premier Krimighna (parasite-destroying) herb. Both bark and seeds show activity against intestinal helminths (roundworms, tapeworms) and Giardia in traditional and experimental settings — often combined with Vidanga and Haridra in classical anti-parasitic protocols.

How to Consume Kurchi

Kurchi is used both as a single herb and as a key ingredient in classical compound formulations. The bark is predominantly used for acute and chronic intestinal conditions; the seeds (Indrayava) are used in fever, malabsorption, and gynaecological conditions. Internal preparations should be taken under the guidance of a qualified Ayurvedic physician.

Safety & Precautions

Kurchi has an excellent safety profile at classical therapeutic doses and is among the most widely used Ayurvedic herbs for gastrointestinal conditions. However, its steroidal alkaloid content (particularly conessine) warrants attention with prolonged high-dose use, as excessive conessine can affect the nervous system.

  • Pregnancy: Use with caution — Indrayava (seeds) are specifically cautioned in early pregnancy due to uterine stimulant properties. Internal use of bark should be supervised by a physician throughout pregnancy.
  • Constipation & dry constitutions: The Ruksha (dry) and Grahi (absorbent) properties can aggravate constipation and Vata dryness; avoid in cases of constipation, hard stools, or very dry Vata constitutions without appropriate anupana (vehicle).
  • Prolonged high-dose use: Chronic high-dose consumption (above classical limits) of conessine-rich preparations may cause neurological symptoms including tremors, confusion, and CNS excitation — adhere to prescribed dose and duration.
  • Sprue & severe malabsorption: While classically used for Grahani, in advanced malabsorption with extreme emaciation, use only under specialist supervision as the drying properties can further deplete tissues.
  • Drug interactions: May potentiate anti-diarrhoeal medications; caution with concomitant use of anti-amoebic drugs (metronidazole) — though traditionally combined, clinical pharmacokinetic interactions are not fully characterised.
  • Children: Traditional paediatric use exists at reduced doses — only under qualified supervision; avoid self-medication in children under 5 years.
  • Hypotension: Steroidal alkaloids at high doses may modestly reduce blood pressure; caution in individuals on antihypertensive therapy.
  • Self-diagnosis of dysentery: Bloody diarrhoea, high fever, or prolonged dysentery require clinical investigation; Kurchi is supportive therapy and not a substitute for diagnosis and emergency care.
